## Optical reading

The writing path is a projection relay, not phase-SLM point-focus holography. Each switched-on DMD micromirror contributes to the projected binary image. The periodic mirror array also separates the broad femtosecond spectrum by angle. At the mask-conjugate build plane, the objective makes those spectral components share the same optical path and spatially overlap; only there is the pulse shortest and its peak intensity highest. Adjacent planes retain temporal spread and lower peak intensity. Layer-by-layer stage motion places successive projected images in the resist.

The native scene therefore uses the real DMD ON/OFF routing and an opt-in wavelength-angle proxy on that same DMD. The relay then carries separately traced wavelengths through L1 and the fold to L2, where they re-overlap on the resin stage. This is useful geometric evidence of the intended conjugate topology, but geometric overlap alone cannot establish the paper’s temporal pulse compression or axial polymerization confinement.

Polarization, the accepted diffraction order, DMD model/pitch, beam expansion, L1 focal length and separations, objective immersion medium/working distance, and any observation path are not specified in the retrieved article. A targeted search of the official article/supplement record on 2026-09-05 exposed the supplement index but did not yield a downloadable supplementary PDF in this runtime; no prescription from an unverified secondary apparatus was substituted.

## Free interpretation — not specified in the paper

- The scene’s 100 mW laser average power exists only to enable native power/ray behavior. It is not inferred from 35/39/42 nW per projected pixel and is not a literature source-power claim.
- L1 uses a 180 mm focal length; the DMD display mask uses 7 mm pitch and 0.58 ON duty; the bounded spectral proxy uses 6° per 100 nm about 800 nm.
- The fold placement is compacted, while maintaining a real reflected optical path.
- The objective uses 3.333 mm EFL to display 60× against OpticalSetup’s 200 mm reference tube lens, 0.28 mm working distance, and oil immersion. Only 60× and NA 1.25 are reported here.
- XYZ stage travel is display-amplified to remain visible on the 2D workbench. It is not the paper’s experimental travel or layer spacing.

## Working default

Open collections/2pp/setups/saha-2019.json. The enabled 800 nm, 35 fs, 0.001 MHz source strikes an ON DMD stripe. The transform-limited spectrum branches into wavelength-dependent geometric paths at the DMD, passes through L1, reflects from the fold mirror, traverses the 60×/NA 1.25 objective proxy, and produces traced write hits on the resin stage. The stage runs an illustrative synchronized XY/Z raster and the resin preview records bounded pulse-arrival markers.

## Control experiments

1. **Laser enable/power control:** clear **Emit traced rays** on the pulsed laser. Expected visible effect: all computed paths and resin write arrivals disappear. Restoring emission returns them. Average power changes power attribution but the scene is not a curing model.
2. **Temporal-focusing mechanism control:** clear **Disperse broadband light** on the DMD. Expected visible effect: the colored angular fan collapses to one wavelength-independent geometry. This demonstrates the role of DMD dispersion in the relay; neither state calculates pulse duration versus depth.
3. **Binary-mask control:** lower **ON fraction** from 0.58 to 0.05. At the default illuminated pattern coordinate, the selected stripe switches OFF and the downstream path/write hit disappears. This is native DMD amplitude routing, not an arbitrary hologram solver.
4. **Layer-motion control:** change the stage from **XYZ sync — raster** to **Static**. Expected visible effect: the specimen stops moving and new preview marks remain at one mounted-sample coordinate. The animated travel is intentionally enlarged and does not reproduce the experimental motion law.

## Model and handoff limits

OpticalSetup computes finite geometric rays, wavelength-dependent DMD exit angles, ideal-lens/mirror/objective interactions, optical-path bookkeeping, and pulsed arrivals at the sample. It does **not** solve a spatiotemporal electric field, arbitrary DMD diffraction, blaze efficiency, a vectorial PSF, pulse-front tilt, temporal focusing, focal-sheet thickness, nonlinear absorption, radical chemistry, dose thresholds, polymer conversion, 151 nm pixels, 175 nm features, or calibrated processing rate.

The paper preset is only partly compatible with the Two-Photon Lithography Lab contract. Wavelength 800 nm and NA 1.25 are supported. Repetition rate 0.001 MHz and pulse duration 35 fs are outside the lab’s accepted ranges and must remain omitted rather than clamped. Source power is unknown and must not be transferred; the scene-only 100 mW interpretation is not a paper preset.

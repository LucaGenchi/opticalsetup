This review assesses its own scene and native modules; no integration improvements are credited. **Browser verification is pending**, at the coordinator's request while the shared browser connection is recovered.

**Primary evidence:** Saha et al., [“Scalable submicrometer additive manufacturing”](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax8760), *Science* 366, 105–109 (2019). Read the supplied six-page PDF and visually inspected rendered p. 1, especially Fig. 1A–C and its apparatus caption. SHA-256: `b29f3e7b928c690852f19316c2adb96e656618fc9109ce30c1c0fa5f27c82e66`. Also read the original evidence note, JSON, DMD/tracer/objective/stage implementation and tests, and the integration style, shared review and image-plane criterion.

The paper supports laser → DMD mask/disperser → collimating L1 → mirror → objective L2 → resist on an XYZ stage (p. 1 Fig. 1B). The mask-to-build-plane conjugacy and temporal-focusing mechanism are explicit on pp. 1–2. The native component order follows that evidence. The 800 nm centre, nominal 35 fs and 60×/NA 1.25 objective are reported in p. 3 Fig. 3; 1 kHz is reported on p. 4. The paper describes roughly 3 ms exposures and 20 ms layer intervals including stage motion, rather than the scene's continuously illuminated raster (p. 4).

The main PDF lacks the supplementary instrument prescription. Source polarization, DMD model/pitch/incidence/order, L1 focal length and detailed separations, objective EFL/WD/medium and transmissions remain unverified. The scene's 180 mm L1, 6 mm beam, 3 mm stripes, 0.58 duty, 12° mirror tilt, 6°/100 nm spectral slope, oil objective and enlarged stage travel are design choices. The inferred 3.333 mm EFL displays 60× against the app's 200 mm reference; the actual 180 mm collecting lens gives approximately 54× geometric demagnification before the tilted-face projection. No observation path is established by the supplied article; the added output dump is clearly labelled.

**Power correction:** p. 3 Fig. 3 measures 35/39/42 nW per projected pixel immediately before the objective aperture. These are correctly excluded as laser-source power. However, p. 4 also reports a commercial 1 kHz laser with approximately **1 mJ pulse energy**. The note's blanket “source power is unknown” statement misses that source specification: multiplying those approximate source values gives approximately 1 W, without establishing the exact operating/attenuated power in any experiment. The native 100 mW remains an explicitly invented setting and must not be exported as a paper preset. The existing paper handoff appropriately supplies only 800 nm and NA 1.25, omitting 35 fs and 0.001 MHz outside the destination's ranges.

**Full-beam image audit:** this requires DMD field-plane → resin imaging, not scanner → objective-BFP imaging. The active DMD face is local x=−9, centred at world `(119.999909, 220.000370)`; L1 is at x=300, the fold at `(400,220)`, the objective equivalent plane at y=303.333 and nominal resin at y=306.666. The intended central distances are 180 / 183.333 / 3.333 mm, giving nominal magnification −3.333/180 and B≈0. This explains the passing central-reference regression but is not a full-field conjugacy result.

I replaced the sized source **in memory only** with independent native line probes at all 25 actual source sample offsets, −3 to +3 mm in 0.25 mm steps, retaining the original directions, spectrum, DMD and downstream optics. Fifteen offsets are ON at duty 0.58, each delivering three spectral samples through the objective. At 800 nm their image coordinates map linearly across 0.101495 mm. Yet each off-centre field point has wavelength-dependent image position: spectral x-span rises from **4.42×10⁻⁷ mm centrally to 0.00081134 mm at the beam edge**. The DMD face is tilted relative to the outgoing relay axis, so its field-dependent distance to L1 changes by approximately ±1.220 mm; the corresponding local image-matrix B reaches approximately ±0.02259 mm. All admitted probes actually intersect the objective equivalent lens. The scene therefore demonstrates central spectral overlap and approximate mask imaging, not the claimed whole-mask spectral conjugacy. The tilted-face error must be addressed before claiming otherwise.

Reproduction uses the original modules with `node --test test/saha-2019-scene.test.js test/dmd-pattern.test.js test/2pp-paper-handoff.test.js` and `node --test --test-name-pattern=DMD test/components.test.js`. The existing Saha tests check central references and never test these full-beam field points.

**Pulse and pattern limits:** transform-limited Gaussian mode derives 26.8986 nm FWHM from the configured 35 fs; the paper only says tens of nanometres (p. 2), so this precise spectrum is inferred. DMD dispersion changes ray directions, not temporal phase. The native thin-lens/objective glass proxies instead accumulate approximately 1503–1615 fs² GDD and report **124–133 fs** at the sample, unchanged at every tested stage depth. Central-ray path bookkeeping also differs by 1.33734 mm between centre and adjacent spectral samples, so it does not validate the equal optical paths described by Fig. 1. Neither quantity is a temporal-focusing calculation or a physical prediction for this apparatus.

The original DMD displays a static 1D stripe section, with no programmed 2D layer frames. Stage “XYZ sync” couples a lateral triangular motion to stepped depth; it does not switch projected masks or synchronize exposures to the paper's layer schedule. Only the source's central spatial reference generates write markers. Geometric spectral overlap, reference arrivals and illustrative depth-dependent marker styling cannot demonstrate a projected 2D layer, axial confinement, 151 nm pixels, polymerization, self-proximity effects or throughput. The scene acknowledges many model limits, but “geometric mask imaging is calculated” and the duty-control explanation need qualification.

**Visual evidence:** inspected the original native `buildSVG()` export rendered to PNG, not an alternative diagram. It has a compact genuine fold, concise component labels and explicit interpretation/limit blocks; it is clearer than the much larger Somers bench at export scale. The 680×410 frame is slightly wider than the collection target, the source points back toward the DMD instead of following the preferred upper-left reading order, and explanatory text is only 8 world units high. The output beam crosses the resin label and the actual image field is tiny beside the large marker. Frame containment passes, but that does not establish editor readability or absence of overlap at fitted browser size.

**Projection-family comparison:** [Somers original review](somers-2021.md) supplies substantially fuller primary Methods, including expansion, conditioning, DMD incidence, collecting focal length and observation/alignment branches. Its independently tested off-axis DMD points retain spectral coincidence at the image plane. Both originals need shared 2D frames, honest pulse/motion semantics and legible limitations; Somers also needs major visual work and completion of its browser checks. Saha's simpler native layout is an advantage, but does not outweigh the weaker prescription evidence and demonstrated full-field defect. This preference follows evidence and native behavior, not publication date.

**DROP** from the five-paper selection if Somers retains the projection-family slot. Keep Saha as a cited predecessor in the narrative. Reconsider as **KEEP AFTER REWORK** only if a deliberately simpler projection example is preferred: resolve field-wide conjugacy, replace reference-only mask feedback with the shared sampled-frame contract, correct source provenance and pulse/geometry wording, and finish both browser layouts and controls. Neither original qualifies for unconditional **KEEP**.

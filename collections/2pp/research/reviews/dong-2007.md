## Primary evidence and its limits

The corrected paper is X.-Z. Dong, Z.-S. Zhao and X.-M. Duan, *Applied Physics
Letters* 91, 124103 (2007), DOI `10.1063/1.2789661`. Its abstract supports
multiple-beam multiphoton processing with geometry selected through lens-set
parameters and aperture masks. It does not establish the implemented
source-to-sample train. [Publisher record](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apl/article/91/12/124103/334552/Micronanofabrication-of-assembled-three).

Fresh exact-title, DOI, author/repository and apparatus searches were made
on the review date. The publisher landing-page request failed in the reading
tool; the indexed [publisher PDF URL](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apl/article-pdf/doi/10.1063/1.2789661/14380186/124103_1_online.pdf)
resolved to a purchase-labelled HTML record containing abstract and
references, not a readable PDF or article body. No apparatus figure or
supplement was inspected. This reproduces the evidence gap in the current
note; it is not a claim that full text is unavailable to every reader.

Search snippets associated with the publisher refer to Fig. 1 and a
100×/NA 1.40 objective. These are leads only: the full paragraph and figure
could not be validated. They must not become reported scene inputs, nor
justify reconstructing a missing objective by guesswork.

The current note handles this distinction substantially better than an
unsupported reconstruction would: its warning is explicit, its numerical
handoff is disabled, and no observation path is invented. However, the
abstract's phrase about a lens set does **not** verify four independent
microlenses focusing directly into resin. The chosen interpretation remains
the chief selection weakness. A generic lens-array teaching scene can be
useful without establishing a research apparatus.

### Complete beam support, independently of the writing markers

The original beam source emits 25 spatial samples. To avoid judging focus
from the four aggregated `writeHits`, I traced 25 separate native line-mode
pulsed-source cases at exactly the same exit heights: source-centre
y=144,144.5,…,156, with the original downstream train unchanged. The results
below therefore measure individual traced intersections, not marker centres.
Every default ray reaches resin; every sampled lenslet bundle has sample
span below `1e-8 mm`.

| Lenslet focus x (mm) | Input samples | Illuminated array x range (mm) | Default sample x range (mm) | Sample x range with f=45 mm | Defocused span (mm) |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 355 | 6 | 352–359.5 | 355–355 | 353.5–356 | 2.5 |
| 365 | 7 | 361–370 | 365–365 | 363.333333–366.333333 | 3.0 |
| 375 | 6 | 371.5–379 | 375–375 | 373.666667–376.166667 | 2.5 |
| 385 | 6 | 380.5–388 | 385–385 | 384–386.5 | 2.5 |

With f=45 mm, the same rays converge at y=310, then diverge to the unchanged
resin at y=325. The original tracer instead reports four writing markers at
x≈354.75,364.833333,374.916667,385.25 with no support width. These unweighted
centroids may lie between actual arrivals. Their small positional changes
are not a measure of the 2.5–3 mm defocused beam widths.

The default four foci are thus real geometric intersections in this ideal
model. The failure is the marker interpretation away from focus, not a
failure of the default lenslet ray bending. The existing defocus regression
only asks that the four marker positions change and remain finite; it does
not test the width that matters.

## Original maintenance versus shared integration

The original shaper contains repeated `d`, `intensity`, `tag`, `wl`, `bw`
and `speckle` object keys. The duplicate assignments use the same expressions
in the inspected paths, so this is conflict-resolution debt rather than
evidence that the default Dong rays have different last-write values. The
more material original issue is unweighted centroid aggregation, which
averages position, optical path and intensity without retaining support.

The integration's shared `arrival-preview.js` selects an actual arrival and
retains the full sampled spread. That addresses the original centroid
problem; this report's scores do not award the original credit for a future
shared fix. The lenslet element itself can use shared infrastructure with
little paper-specific maintenance.

An additional integration-only regression was reported to the coordinator:
running the original test's four lenslets followed by grating orders `-1,1`
against the inspected integration modules produced **four** arrival groups,
instead of the original's correct **eight**. The new grouping preserved
lenslet identity but combined the two downstream orders into one support
span. Retaining order/branch identity is required alongside the improved
actual-arrival representation. This finding does not reduce the original
branch's score and no shared code was edited by this reviewer.

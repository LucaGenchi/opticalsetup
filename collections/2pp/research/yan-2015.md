## Native scene and provenance

Reported sequence represented: phase-modulating SLM → Fourier-plane zeroth-order filtration → objective focal-plane array → parallel writing of a resin target.

Free interpretation — not specified in the paper:

- 800 nm, 150 fs, 80 MHz, 0.1 W pulsed source. These values only make the native scene run; they are not entries in the paper evidence table or paper-based handoff.
- Transmissive SLM orientation and equivalent `-3,-1,1,3` grating orders. The native shaper computes four angular branches plus a zeroth-order branch; it does not solve the reported CGH or a hexagonal 2D field.
- 60 mm Fourier/relay lenses, 70 lines/mm equivalent phase-gradient strength, 10% zeroth-order fraction, and the 6 mm beam dump.
- 45° packing mirror, 40 mm objective EFL, air operation, NA 0.8, 10 mm working distance, and all spacings.
- Animated 8 mm × 4 mm serpentine piezo raster and generic photocurable resin. Scan hardware and travel are not established by the accessible evidence.

The fold is included only to make a compact, readable figure. It is a real native mirror and the traced optical path follows it.

## What the working default demonstrates

The enabled pulsed source reaches the SLM. Its configured optical function creates four symmetric nonzero orders and one zeroth order. The Fourier lens maps these angular branches toward the Fourier plane, where the beam dump removes the central zeroth order. A second lens and a real fold mirror route the four surviving branches through the objective. All four produce signal/write hits on the resin stage. Stage motion changes the sample position relative to those computed paths.

The 2D scene represents one row of the paper's 4×4 hexagonal array. It does not prove a 16-focus intensity distribution, spot uniformity, CGH phase accuracy, a vectorial focal field, polymerization threshold, dose, cure kinetics, voxel size, scan calibration, throughput, or parabolic-mirror geometry.

## Control experiments

1. **Laser interlock:** select `Illustrative fs source` and turn off **Emit traced rays**. Expected: all orange paths and resin hits disappear. This proves the marks are driven by the native source trace.
2. **Parallelization control:** select `SLM: 4-focus row proxy`, edit the grating layer's orders from `-3,-1,1,3` to `-1,1`. Expected: the computed sample-hit count falls from four to two. This isolates the throughput mechanism represented by parallel Fourier orders.
3. **Zeroth-order rejection:** keep **0th-order reflection** enabled and move or delete `0th-order stop`. Expected: the central undiffracted branch continues through the relay, adding a central sample hit. This shows that the stop removes a computed ray rather than decorating the diagram.
4. **Motion control:** pause/play mechanics or change the stage from **XYZ sync — raster** to **Static**. Expected: the four optical paths remain, while the mounted resin and voxel preview stop moving. This separates parallel focus generation from the free-interpretation scan trajectory.

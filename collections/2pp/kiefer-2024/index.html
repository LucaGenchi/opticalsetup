Current understanding

The microlens array expands beamlet separation without the large chromatic spread that direct high-angle DOE splitting would cause. It is a separate optic after the DOE.

Reported optical sequence

  1. Chameleon Ultra II: 790 nm, 140 fs sech², 80 MHz, 3.7 W
  2. L1/L2 1.25× demagnification to AOM; first order used
  3. L3/L4 1.60× relay to DOE (571 µm beam diameter)
  4. 7×7 DOE, initial separation parameter M≈3
  5. L5/L6 3.33× telescope and L7
  6. Custom aspheric microlens array: approximately 720 µm beam diameter; raises M to 300
  7. LG1 collimation to GX; LG2/LG3 unity relay to GY
  8. LG4/LG5 2× pupil relay
  9. Zeiss 40× NA 1.4 oil objective
  10. 49 foci, 60 µm separation; XYZ specimen positioning

Observation and auxiliary paths

LED transmission illumination above the specimen; a beam splitter (BS) and L8 feed the camera through the writing objective.

Limits and unresolved details

The native lenslet element is a 1D paraxial proxy. It cannot reproduce the custom aspheric prescription, 7×7 vectorial focus or measured efficiency.

  • 954 mW is before the objective for all 49 foci, not 954 mW per focus.
  • 19.5 mW per focus is before the objective; 70% objective transmission is assumed in the paper.
  • No prism pair is added merely because Hahn uses one.

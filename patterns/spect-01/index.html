The example

An illustrative 25 µm slit, 150 mm collimator, 600 lines/mm grating and 150 mm camera optic map a narrow visible band onto a detector. The two mirror legs keep entrance and output separate.

  1. Locate the slit and detector conjugates through both reflective legs.
  2. Double slit width conceptually and compare throughput with overlapping wavelength images.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 25 µm entrance slit (Slit) — Defines the spatial input and resolution/throughput tradeoff.
  2. 150 mm collimator (Concave mirror) — Collimates the slit output before dispersion.
  3. 600 lines/mm grating (Diffraction grating) — Disperses wavelengths in the selected first order.
  4. 150 mm camera optic (Concave mirror) — Focuses angular dispersion onto the detector.
  5. Spectral focal plane (Camera) — Pixel coordinate becomes wavelength after calibration.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Czerny-Turner, Littrow, or related dispersive spectrograph layouts.

Key constraints

Slit width, dispersion, aberrations, and detector sampling trade resolution against throughput.

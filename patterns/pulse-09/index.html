The example

Focus illustrative 1064 nm pulses into a phase-matched crystal, collimate the 532 nm output and use a dichroic plus bandpass to reject the residual fundamental.

  1. Use energy conservation to distinguish the half-wavelength harmonic from a fluorescence band.
  2. Check phase matching and pump rejection independently; geometric overlap does not establish efficient conversion.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 1064 nm pulses (Pulsed Laser) — Energy and bandwidth are chosen within the real crystal acceptance.
  2. Focusing lens (Thin convex lens) — Waist position sets interaction intensity and walk-off tradeoffs.
  3. Phase-matched crystal (Crystal) — The chosen SHG interaction generates 532 nm.
  4. Post-crystal collimator (Thin convex lens) — Collects and collimates the harmonic before spectral separation.
  5. 532 / 1064 nm dichroic (Dichroic mirror) — Separates converted light from the residual pump.
  6. 532 nm bandpass output (Filter) — Cleanup filtering supplies the useful harmonic.
  7. 1064 nm pump dump (Beam dump) — Residual fundamental is excluded.

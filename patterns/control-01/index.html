CONTROL-01 · Control architecture

Power stabilization loop

Measure a beam fraction and correct delivered optical power through feedback.

The example

A 532 nm beam passes an AOM before a 5% monitoring tap; a PI controller compares detector voltage with a setpoint and adjusts RF amplitude.

  1. Identify why the monitor must sit after the actuator.
  2. Explain how downstream losses can drift even while the monitor stays constant.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 532 nm source (CW Laser) — Input power can drift.
  2. Power actuator (AOM) — Vary transmitted/diffracted power using RF amplitude.
  3. 5% monitor tap (Beamsplitter) — Sample power after the actuator.
  4. Experiment beam — Main stabilized delivery path.
  5. Monitor diode (Photodetector) — Detector responsivity defines the error scale.
  6. PI controller — Drive negative feedback from measured minus target power.
  7. Power setpoint — Example target 10 mW at the calibrated delivery plane.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Pickoff detector, controller, and source or attenuator actuator.

Key constraints

Sensor placement, loop bandwidth, actuator range, and out-of-loop drift limit stability.

Fourier-plane filtering

The example

A circular stop at the common Fourier plane of a 100/100 mm relay suppresses high spatial frequencies of a test image.

  1. Locate the filter between the two lenses, rather than in an image plane.
  2. Compare low-pass central aperture selection with a central-blocking high-pass mask.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Line-pair target — Object plane contains fine and coarse features.
  2. 100 mm lens (Thin convex lens) — Converts object structure to a Fourier-plane distribution.
  3. Fourier-plane stop — Central 1 mm radius aperture passes low spatial frequencies.
  4. 100 mm lens (Thin convex lens) — Transforms the filtered field back into an image.
  5. Filtered image (Camera) — Fine edges soften as the aperture contracts.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Imaging or Fourier-transform optics with an amplitude or phase mask at the proper plane.

Key constraints

Mask scale and location depend on wavelength and focal length; useful signal can be rejected.

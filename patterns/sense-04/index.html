The example

A 660 nm spot is observed by a camera displaced 50 mm from the laser axis; a calibrated pixel shift encodes target height.

  1. Change target height while keeping the camera baseline fixed.
  2. Explain why specular surfaces can remove the observed spot.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 660 nm spot (CW Laser) — Narrow illumination line or spot.
  2. Projection lens (Thin convex lens) — Place the illuminated spot near a 200 mm working distance.
  3. Height target — Move target ±10 mm along the illumination axis.
  4. Offset view lens (Thin convex lens) — Observe from a 50 mm lateral baseline.
  5. Centroid camera (Camera) — Track the spot coordinate.
  6. Height calibration — Known heights map pixels to physical displacement.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Projector-camera or multi-camera geometry with calibrated baseline and imaging.

Key constraints

Occlusion, calibration, reflectance, and baseline trade range against precision.

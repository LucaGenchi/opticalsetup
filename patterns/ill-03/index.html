Epi-illumination

Share an objective between illumination and signal collection.

The example

A 500 nm longpass dichroic reflects 488 nm excitation into a shared objective and transmits nominal 520 nm fluorescence toward the camera.

  1. Follow excitation and emission through the shared objective in opposite directions.
  2. Identify the additional emission filter rather than relying only on the dichroic cutoff.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 488 nm excitation (CW Laser) — Clean the excitation spectrum before injection.
  2. 500 nm dichroic (Dichroic mirror) — Reflect short wavelengths, transmit long wavelengths.
  3. Shared objective (Objective) — Excitation and collection use the same objective.
  4. Fluorescent specimen (Sample) — Chosen emission near 520 nm.
  5. 520 nm emission filter (Filter) — Reject residual excitation before detection.
  6. Emission camera (Camera) — Read the emission branch.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Illumination separator, shared objective, and filtered detection branch.

Key constraints

Excitation leakage, back-reflections, and spectral separation limit weak-signal collection.

Phase-to-intensity conversion

Modify the relative phase of reference and scattered fields to create intensity contrast.

The example

A 550 nm illumination annulus is conjugate to a phase ring in a 40× objective pupil. Direct light receives a nominal quarter-wave phase shift while specimen-scattered light mostly bypasses the ring.

  1. Follow the two pupil-plane contributions and locate their recombination.
  2. Misregister the annulus and ring conceptually; identify the resulting unwanted reference leakage.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Condenser annulus — Forms a hollow cone matched to the objective phase ring.
  2. Transparent cell (Sample) — Optical-path variations scatter part of the illumination.
  3. Direct pupil ring (Phase object) — Reference field receives nominal π/2 phase bias.
  4. Scattered pupil field — Spatially displaced field largely misses the ring.
  5. Tube lens (Thin convex lens) — Recombines reference and scattered fields into an image.
  6. Phase-contrast image (Camera) — Intensity carries phase-dependent contrast.

Design choices

Holographic trap arrays

Create and reposition multiple trapping sites through spatial beam control.

The example

A phase SLM divides an 850 nm trap beam into four spots relayed to the objective pupil; fluorescence images report site occupancy.

  1. Inspect the pupil relay before interpreting the target spot pitch.
  2. Separate site brightness from evidence of single-atom occupation.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 850 nm trap (CW Laser) — Common trap illumination.
  2. Four-site hologram (SLM) — Target a 2×2 array at 5 µm pitch.
  3. Pupil relay (Thin convex lens) — Conjugate SLM to objective pupil.
  4. High-NA objective (Objective) — Form array in the atom plane.
  5. Four trap sites — Specified spatial target, not a computed potential.
  6. Occupancy camera (Camera) — Collect filtered fluorescence.
  7. Hologram update — Use observed uniformity to revise phase pattern.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

SLM holograms, diffractive splitting, or time-shared deflection with focusing optics.

Key constraints

Power uniformity, aberrations, crosstalk, and trap dynamics constrain scaling.

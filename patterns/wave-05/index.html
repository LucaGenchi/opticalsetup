Coronagraphic rejection

Suppress a bright source through coordinated field and pupil-plane operations.

The example

A bright on-axis star and faint off-axis companion enter a relay. An illustrative opaque focal mask covers the stellar core; a reimaged pupil stop rejects diffracted edge light before the final science focus.

  1. Distinguish the first focal mask from the pupil-plane stop.
  2. Offset the star from mask center conceptually and identify leakage without assuming the companion also brightens.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Star + off-axis companion — Angular separation is mapped to different focal positions.
  2. First stellar focus (Thin convex lens) — Place the occulting mask in a field plane.
  3. Focal occulting mask (Invisible blocker) — Blocks the bright stellar core while passing off-axis light.
  4. Reimaged-pupil Lyot stop (Slit) — Rejects diffracted pupil-edge light; illustrative diameter is 90% of pupil.
  5. Science focal plane (Camera) — Companion throughput and stellar leakage must both be measured.
  6. Occulted stellar light (Beam dump) — Bright rejected light can feed diagnostics or a dump.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Focal-plane masks and Lyot stops; apodized, phase-mask, or related variants.

Key constraints

Wavefront errors, finite source size, alignment, and chromaticity limit rejection.

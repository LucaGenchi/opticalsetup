ACCESS-06 · Optical layout

Side-viewing rotational probe

Acquire circumferential views through a lateral beam direction and probe rotation.

The example

A rotary joint feeds a fiber/GRIN assembly and a side-reflecting distal prism. Rotation sweeps a circumferential tissue cross-section; an external reference arm supplies OCT depth discrimination.

  1. Separate radial depth acquisition from circumferential rotation and axial pullback.
  2. Identify sheath refraction and nonuniform rotation as distortions requiring calibration.

Follow the arrangement

  1. OCT sample-arm input — Broadband interferometric illumination arrives from the console.
  2. Rotary fiber joint — Couples light into a rotating fiber path.
  3. Distal GRIN lens — Focuses the fiber output toward the side reflector.
  4. Side-view prism (Prism) — Turns the beam radially through a transparent sheath.
  5. Circumferential wall (Sample) — Angular scans sample the surrounding tissue.
  6. OCT interferometric receiver — Round-trip sample light meets an external reference.
  7. Rotation encoder — Associates each depth scan with an azimuthal angle.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Fiber delivery, distal focusing, angled reflector or prism, and rotational scan.

Key constraints

Rotation nonuniformity, sheath refraction, working distance, and motion affect reconstruction.

The example

An illustrative 1064 nm resonator stores inversion with an acousto-optic loss gate active, then rapidly lowers loss to produce a pulse through a fixed output coupler.

  1. Distinguish stored inversion from stored circulating optical energy.
  2. Keep the output coupler fixed while changing the loss gate; identify why this differs from cavity dumping.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Pump source — Builds population inversion while oscillation is suppressed.
  2. Nd:YAG gain (Crystal) — Energy is stored in excited populations.
  3. High reflector (Mirror) — Closes one cavity end.
  4. Acousto-optic loss gate (AOM) — High-loss then low-loss switching changes cavity Q.
  5. Fixed output coupler (Mirror) — Transmits a constant fraction during the pulse.
  6. Nanosecond pulse port — Pulse duration depends on dynamics, not just gate width.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Gain resonator with actively or passively controlled intracavity loss.

Key constraints

Gain dynamics and switching determine pulse formation; energy is initially stored in the medium.

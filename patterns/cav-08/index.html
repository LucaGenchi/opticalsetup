The example

An illustrative 80 MHz ring includes a pumped gain segment, fast saturable loss, negative-dispersion mirrors and a small output coupler. Total group delay, not diagram length, fixes the repetition rate.

  1. Identify the distinct roles of gain, saturable loss and dispersion.
  2. Compare 12.5 ns pulse spacing with a femtosecond pulse duration; these are different time scales.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Pumped gain segment (Crystal) — Balances round-trip loss.
  2. Saturable loss — Favors short high-intensity pulses over weak background.
  3. Dispersion mirrors (Pulse compressor) — Balance accumulated group-delay dispersion.
  4. Output coupler (Mirror) — Extracts a small fraction of each circulation.
  5. 80 MHz pulse train (Pulsed Laser) — Nominal separation is 12.5 ns.
  6. Ring return optics (Mirror) — Close the resonator without resetting the pulse state.
  7. Pump input — Supplies gain energy independently of output pulses.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Resonator with active modulation or passive mode-locking and dispersion management.

Key constraints

Mode locking depends on gain, loss, nonlinearity, and dispersion balance.

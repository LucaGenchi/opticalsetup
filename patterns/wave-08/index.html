Stray-light baffling

The example

A camera uses a field stop at an intermediate image and blackened vanes around the accepted beam. A deliberate ghost reflection is routed to a dump rather than toward the detector.

  1. Follow the accepted beam through the field stop and contrast it with the intercepted ghost.
  2. Move a vane inward conceptually and check marginal field bundles before claiming better rejection.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Accepted scene field — The required field and pupil determine allowed ray bundles.
  2. Front lens group (Lens group) — Forms an intermediate image and can produce unwanted ghosts.
  3. Intermediate field stop (Slit) — Rejects out-of-field images without clipping the desired field.
  4. Baffled relay tube (Lens group) — Vanes intercept off-path scatter; vane positions must respect the pupil.
  5. Sensitive detector (Camera) — Only accepted optical paths should reach this surface.
  6. Ghost / wall scatter — Unwanted path starts at a surface or illuminated tube wall.
  7. Blackened interception (Beam dump) — A controlled absorber stops the unwanted path.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Field stops, vanes, beam dumps, blackened surfaces, and controlled apertures.

Key constraints

Baffles must reject unwanted paths without clipping the required field or adding scatter.

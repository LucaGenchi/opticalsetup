PULSE-05 · Measurement architecture

Pump-probe

Prepare a sample and interrogate its response at a controlled delay.

The example

Split illustrative 800 nm, 100 fs pulses into pump and probe arms. A 150 µm stage move changes the retroreflected pump delay by about 1 ps; a probe reference normalizes the transmission signal.

  1. Calculate 2 × 150 µm/c ≈ 1 ps and separate stage position from sample time zero.
  2. Check the probe reference and pump rejection before interpreting a differential transmission signal.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 800 nm pulse source (Pulsed Laser) — Common origin provides a repeatable time relation.
  2. Pump / probe split (Beamsplitter) — Arms have different roles, not necessarily equal power.
  3. Pump retro-delay (Mechanical delay line) — Round-trip delay changes by 2Δx/c.
  4. Probe reference tap (Beamsplitter) — Samples the probe arm before the specimen.
  5. Probe reference detector (Photodetector) — Measures probe fluctuations before interaction.
  6. Overlap at specimen (Sample) — Pump perturbs the sample and probe interrogates it.
  7. Probe transmission (Photodetector) — Selects the probe channel and rejects pump leakage.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Split or synchronized pulses, variable delay, sample overlap, and selective detection.

Key constraints

Timing jitter, overlap, pulse widths, and sample recovery set temporal interpretation.

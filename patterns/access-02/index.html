Coherent image-bundle transport

Transfer spatial image samples through an ordered collection of fiber cores.

The example

A distal objective maps a tissue field onto a coherent fiber bundle. At the proximal end a magnifying relay images the core pattern onto a camera; a separate illumination fiber lights the tissue.

  1. Follow one distal core to its corresponding proximal location.
  2. Compare a feature smaller than a core spacing with a resolved feature; coherent ordering does not eliminate sampling limits.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Tissue field (Sample) — Continuous spatial detail is sampled by discrete fiber cores.
  2. Distal objective (Objective) — Matches the specimen field to the bundle face.
  3. Coherent image bundle — Maintains core-to-core spatial ordering from distal to proximal face.
  4. Proximal magnifying relay (Lens group) — Resolves the bundle output core pattern on the sensor.
  5. Proximal camera (Camera) — Records a sampled image, including bundle texture.
  6. Separate illumination fiber — Provides light without mixing the image-core mapping.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Distal imaging optics and an ordered fiber bundle with proximal readout.

Key constraints

Coherent here means spatial order, not phase locking; core sampling and crosstalk limit images.

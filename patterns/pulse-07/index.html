PULSE-07 · Measurement architecture

Autocorrelation and cross-correlation

Measure overlap between delayed pulse replicas or different pulses.

Schematic exampleBackground-free noncollinear autocorrelator

Pulse splitter — Both replicas come from the same pulse.

Open in full canvas ↗Download setup

Diagram scope: No nonlinear temporal overlap or deconvolution is computed; an autocorrelation alone does not determine full spectral phase. The editable diagram contains annotations and connection arrows; it does not run an optical simulation.

The example

Split an illustrative 800 nm pulse into two equally polarized arms, delay one and cross them in a thin SHG crystal. Detect only the sum-frequency overlap direction while dumping the individual arms.

  1. Trace the overlap-generated channel and both rejected individual channels.
  2. Distinguish measured correlation width from pulse width; deconvolution depends on an assumed pulse shape.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Pulse splitter (Beamsplitter) — Both replicas come from the same pulse.
  2. Fixed replica arm (Mirror) — Defines reference arrival time.
  3. Variable replica delay (Mechanical delay line) — Scans the relative arrival time.
  4. Noncollinear SHG overlap (Crystal) — Overlap generates a correlation-dependent 400 nm signal.
  5. 400 nm spatial / spectral selection (Filter) — Separates cross-generated SHG from fundamental and single-arm backgrounds.
  6. Integrated SHG detector (Photodetector) — Records correlation signal versus delay.
  7. 800 nm dumps (Beam dump) — Individual transmitted replicas are excluded from the readout.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Split-delay-recombine geometry with nonlinear or gated detection.

Key constraints

An autocorrelation alone generally does not uniquely determine pulse shape or phase.

COMPUTE-07 · Measurement architecture

Phase-diversity acquisition

Use images with known optical differences to estimate image and wavefront jointly.

Schematic exampleSimultaneous focused and defocused images

The example

A 50:50 split records one nominal-focus image and one image with known 1 mm axial defocus; a shared solver estimates object and aberration.

  1. Keep the unknown aberration common to both branches.
  2. Explain why two arbitrary blurred images do not constitute calibrated phase diversity.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Unknown object — One scene observed simultaneously in both branches.
  2. Common objective (Objective) — Both branches inherit the same upstream aberrations.
  3. Image split (Beamsplitter) — Create focused and defocused acquisition paths.
  4. Focus camera (Camera) — Nominal conjugate plane.
  5. Diversity solver — Fit common object and pupil using known diversity.
  6. +1 mm camera (Camera) — Second camera moved from nominal image plane.
  7. Defocus calibration — Measure the actual introduced pupil phase.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Simultaneous or sequential images with known defocus or other diversity.

Key constraints

Model accuracy, diversity choice, and scene content affect identifiability.

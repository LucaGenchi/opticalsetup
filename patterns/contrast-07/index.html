The example

An illustrative 920 nm, 100 fs, 80 MHz source is precompensated, scanned and focused through a 20× water objective. A near-objective pickoff collects emission without a confocal pinhole.

  1. Identify why emission need not traverse the scan or a pinhole.
  2. Hold average power fixed and discuss how pulse broadening reduces peak intensity and nonlinear signal.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 920 nm pulses (Pulsed Laser) — Pulse parameters are design inputs, not predicted sample intensity.
  2. Precompensation (Pulse compressor) — Opposes dispersion before the objective.
  3. Raster scanner (Galvo mirror) — Steers the focal spot across the specimen.
  4. Water objective (Objective) — High numerical aperture localizes nonlinear excitation.
  5. Focal specimen (Sample) — Two-photon probability depends on local intensity squared.
  6. Emission pickoff (Dichroic mirror) — Collects close to the objective before descanning.
  7. Non-descanned PMT (Photomultiplier (PMT)) — No pinhole is used in this detection branch.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Pulsed excitation focused by an objective with suitable collection or processing geometry.

Key constraints

Pulse delivery, focal intensity, and sample response govern localization and damage.

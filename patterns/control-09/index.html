CONTROL-09 · Optical layout

Calibration-source injection

Introduce a known optical reference through a repeatable instrument path.

Schematic exampleSwitchable line-lamp spectrometer calibration

Sample emission — Unknown spectrum delivered to the instrument.

Diagram scope: Line fitting, wavelength calibration and spectrometer drift are not computed; the example wavelengths are Hg lines rather than simulated outputs. The editable diagram contains annotations and connection arrows; it does not run an optical simulation.

The example

A shuttered sample input and a mercury line lamp share one entrance slit; the spectrometer records calibration lines in the same dispersive path as sample spectra.

  1. Compare lamp and sample slit illumination before sharing a calibration.
  2. Use an additional line to test fit residual rather than validating only fitted points.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Sample emission (Point source) — Unknown spectrum delivered to the instrument.
  2. Input selector (Mirror) — Alternate sample and standard; do not superpose by accident.
  3. Shared slit (Slit) — Preserve entrance position between calibration and measurement.
  4. Dispersive instrument (Diffraction grating) — Common optical path minimizes geometric differences.
  5. Spectrum camera (Camera) — Locate detector coordinates of spectral lines.
  6. Mercury line lamp (Point source) — Example calibration lines 435.8 and 546.1 nm.
  7. Pixel/wavelength fit — Fit a calibration map and retain residuals.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Source selector or injection port with optics reproducing relevant instrument illumination.

Key constraints

Calibration and science paths may differ in pupil fill, spectrum, polarization, or stability.

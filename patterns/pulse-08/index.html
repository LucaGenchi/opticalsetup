The example

A delayed replica gates an illustrative 800 nm pulse in a thin SHG crystal. A spectrometer records the 400 nm signal at every delay; reconstruction must reproduce the complete measured trace.

  1. Compare the 2D delay–wavelength trace with the integrated autocorrelation.
  2. Require a reconstruction residual and understand SHG-FROG direction-of-time ambiguity before interpreting the result.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Replica splitter (Beamsplitter) — Creates pulse and gate from the same source.
  2. Pulse arm (Mirror) — Carries the waveform to be characterized.
  3. Gate delay arm (Mechanical delay line) — Scans temporal overlap.
  4. Thin SHG crystal (Crystal) — Nonlinear overlap supplies the gate.
  5. SHG spectrometer (Diffraction grating) — Measures wavelength-resolved gated light.
  6. Delay × wavelength trace (Camera) — A sequence of spectra forms the 2D trace.
  7. Phase-retrieval check — Reconstruct a pulse and compare its forward trace with the data.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

FROG uses a spectrally resolved gate; SPIDER uses spectral-shear interferometry.

Key constraints

Each realization has its own sampling, calibration, and retrieval assumptions.

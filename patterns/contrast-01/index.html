The example

A 488 nm raster beam excites a specimen through a 40× objective; the return is descanned before a conjugate pinhole and PMT. Choose a nominal one-Airy-unit aperture at 520 nm.

  1. Trace the shared outgoing and returning path and identify where the scan is removed.
  2. Compare a one-Airy-unit aperture with two Airy units; describe the signal/sectioning tradeoff.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 488 nm excitation (CW Laser) — Continuous-wave excitation starts the scanned channel.
  2. Dichroic (Dichroic mirror) — Reflect excitation and transmit the longer-wavelength return.
  3. Scan / descan (Galvo mirror) — The same scanner removes scan motion from returning fluorescence.
  4. 40× objective (Objective) — Excitation and collection share a diffraction-limited focal region.
  5. Focal specimen (Sample) — Only fluorescence conjugate to the aperture is efficiently collected.
  6. Conjugate pinhole (Slit) — Nominal one Airy unit at 520 nm; opening it trades sectioning for signal.
  7. Emission PMT (Photomultiplier (PMT)) — Measures the descanned, aperture-selected fluorescence.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Focused illumination, collection optics, and pinhole or fiber detection.

Key constraints

Pinhole size trades throughput against sectioning; scattering degrades selectivity.

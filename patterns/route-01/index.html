ROUTE-01 · Pattern family

Wavelength combining and splitting

Route different spectral bands through shared or separate optical paths.

Ray exampleTwo-color dichroic splitter

488 + 640 nm input — Two wavelengths share one incident axis.

Open in full canvas ↗Download setup

Ray model: The dichroic model uses ideal wavelength bands; angle-dependent coating spectra, transition width and leakage are absent.

The example

Coaxial 488 and 640 nm beams encounter a 550 nm longpass dichroic: red transmits and blue reflects into a second detector.

  1. Open the traced scene and read the wavelength at each detector.
  2. Move the cutoff below 488 nm or above 640 nm to send both colors to the same port.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 488 + 640 nm input (CW Laser) — Two wavelengths share one incident axis.
  2. 550 nm longpass (Dichroic mirror) — At 45°, short wavelengths are reflected.
  3. 640 nm detector (Photodetector) — Transmitted long-wavelength channel.
  4. 488 nm detector (Photodetector) — Reflected short-wavelength channel.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Dichroic trees, filter cascades, or dispersive separators.

Key constraints

Band edges shift with incidence and polarization; leakage and loss accumulate.

Distal scanning

Scan the beam at the probe tip instead of relaying a full image.

The example

A single-mode delivery fiber is driven near its tip to sweep a focused spot over tissue. Surrounding return fibers feed a proximal detector; synchronized tip motion supplies the image coordinate.

  1. Locate the moving actuator at the distal tip and compare it with proximal bundle addressing.
  2. Explain why the detector requires calibrated scan timing even though collection fibers need no image ordering.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Proximal laser (CW Laser) — Supplies the excitation through a delivery fiber.
  2. Delivery fiber — Carries light to the distal moving tip.
  3. Piezo-driven distal tip — The scan occurs at the probe end, not at the proximal face.
  4. Distal micro-objective (Objective) — Maps tip deflection to a specimen spot.
  5. Scanned tissue (Sample) — A time sequence samples the surface.
  6. Surrounding return fibers — Separate nonimaging collection channels gather reflected light.
  7. Proximal detector (Photodetector) — Signal is assigned to synchronized distal scan coordinates.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Vibrating fiber, MEMS mirror, or other distal deflector with collection channels.

Key constraints

Probe diameter, scanner trajectory, calibration, and collection efficiency limit performance.

The example

An illustrative 589 nm sodium guide beacon is sensed separately from a near-infrared science target. A shared pupil corrector handles inferred high-order aberrations; a natural-star channel supplies tip/tilt information.

  1. Compare finite-height guide rays with the science-star geometry.
  2. Identify why a sodium beacon cannot supply every atmospheric correction degree of freedom by itself.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 589 nm guide beacon — Finite-altitude guide light samples a different volume from starlight.
  2. NIR science star — Science source is effectively at infinity.
  3. Common pupil DM (Deformable mirror) — Applies commands inferred from guide sensing.
  4. Guide / science split (Dichroic mirror) — Separates guide wavelength from science.
  5. Guide wavefront sensor — Measures high-order guide-star information.
  6. Science camera (Camera) — Records the astronomical target.
  7. Natural-star tip/tilt — Absolute tip/tilt is constrained by an additional reference.

Stable optical resonator

Recirculate a supported optical mode through a chosen sequence of surfaces.

The example

Choose L = 300 mm and two R = 500 mm concave mirrors. The paraxial stability product is g₁g₂ = (1 − L/R)² = 0.16; a weak transmission port samples the cavity.

  1. Compute g₁g₂ and compare it with the stable interval 0 < g₁g₂ < 1.
  2. Increase L to 1000 mm and identify the marginal boundary; geometrical stability alone does not prove useful coupling.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Mode-matched input (CW Laser) — A seed is coupled through the first mirror.
  2. R = 500 mm input mirror (Concave mirror) — Partial transmission couples the input; curvature defines g₁.
  3. Central waist plane — Symmetry places the nominal fundamental-mode waist here.
  4. R = 500 mm end mirror (Concave mirror) — Returns light for a 300 mm geometrical round trip leg.
  5. Transmission monitor (Photodetector) — A small transmitted field diagnoses resonance.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Linear, ring, and bow-tie geometries with suitable focusing and coupling.

Key constraints

Geometric stability, losses, dispersion, and mode selection must be checked together.

COMPUTE-03 · Measurement architecture

Ptychographic acquisition

Use overlapping illuminated regions to constrain diffraction-based reconstruction.

The example

A coherent 10 µm probe scans a 40×40 µm sample with 4 µm steps; each overlapping position produces a far-field diffraction frame.

  1. Calculate the nominal overlap along an adjacent scan direction.
  2. Explain why an intensity frame alone does not reveal its optical phase.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Coherent probe (CW Laser) — Example 532 nm laboratory source.
  2. 10 µm probe (Thin convex lens) — Define illuminated patch size.
  3. XY specimen stage (Sample on piezo stage) — Move by 4 µm between adjacent positions.
  4. Diffraction camera (Camera) — Capture intensity in a distant/Fourier plane.
  5. Iterative recovery — Jointly estimate object and probe from overlapping frames.
  6. Position record — Attach measured scan coordinates to every frame.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Controlled overlapping scan, diffraction detector, and iterative reconstruction.

Key constraints

Overlap, position accuracy, coherence, and model validity determine reconstruction reliability.

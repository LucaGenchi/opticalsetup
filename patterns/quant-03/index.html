Two-photon interference

Overlap photons at a beam splitter and compare output coincidence statistics.

The example

Two nominally identical 810 nm photons enter opposite ports of a 50:50 beamsplitter; a delay scan compares coincident output clicks.

  1. Trace both input and both output ports.
  2. List distinguishability changes that would reduce an experimentally measured dip.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Photon A — First independently heralded input.
  2. Variable delay (Mechanical delay line) — Sweep ±1 ps around temporal overlap.
  3. 50:50 splitter (Beamsplitter) — Two-input interference junction.
  4. Counter 1 (Photodetector) — One output counter.
  5. Coincidence record — Plot coincidences against the proposed delay.
  6. Photon B — Match spectrum and polarization to photon A.
  7. Counter 2 (Photodetector) — Other output counter.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Two prepared input modes, adjustable overlap/delay, splitter, and coincidence detection.

Key constraints

Temporal, spectral, spatial, and polarization distinguishability change the result.

Cold-stop matching

Restrict an infrared detector's optical view with a cooled pupil stop.

The example

A teaching 3–5 µm camera reimages its entrance pupil onto an 80 K cold stop. The stop matches the illuminated pupil so the detector sees the intended scene while warm structure outside the pupil is excluded.

  1. Match pupil diameter and position at the cold stop before judging background suppression.
  2. Enlarge the stop conceptually and identify the added warm view; shrink it and identify desired light loss.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 3–5 µm scene — Desired infrared radiance enters the designed aperture.
  2. Entrance pupil — Defines the intended detector view.
  3. Pupil reimaging optics (Lens group) — Conjugates entrance pupil onto the cold-stop plane.
  4. 80 K matched stop (Slit) — A pupil mask excludes warm surrounding surfaces.
  5. Cooled infrared sensor (Camera) — Integrates accepted scene plus remaining thermal background.
  6. Warm housing path — Undesired thermal radiance is geometrically excluded.
  7. Cold-stop rejection — A stop mismatch can admit background or clip scene light.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Relay the system pupil onto a cold aperture in the detector optical train.

Key constraints

Pupil mismatch clips desired light or admits unwanted thermal emission.

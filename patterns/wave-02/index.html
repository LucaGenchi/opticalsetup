Multi-conjugate correction

The example

Choose teaching conjugates at ground level and 9 km. Three guide-star measurements constrain a tomographic estimate that drives two separately conjugated mirrors across an extended science field.

  1. Identify the two optical conjugates and the multiple sensing directions.
  2. Compare a well-covered central region with an extrapolated field edge; two DMs alone do not guarantee uniform correction.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Extended science field — Different directions encounter different layered aberrations.
  2. Ground-conjugate DM (Deformable mirror) — Corrects the inferred low-altitude layer.
  3. 9 km conjugate DM (Deformable mirror) — A relay conjugates this corrector to a high layer.
  4. Wide-field camera (Camera) — Science quality must be checked across the field.
  5. Three guide directions — Angular diversity samples different atmospheric paths.
  6. Guide wavefront sensors — Independent slope measurements constrain the layer estimate.
  7. Layer tomography — Separates estimates before commanding each DM.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Several guide measurements and correctors conjugated to different depths or altitudes.

Key constraints

Tomographic inference, conjugation accuracy, and guide coverage determine corrected field.

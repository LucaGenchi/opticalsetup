XRAY-06 · Measurement architecture

Propagation-based phase contrast

Convert sample-induced phase structure into intensity through propagation.

The example

An illustrative coherent 20 keV beam crosses a weakly absorbing specimen. Compare a near-contact detector position with a 0.5 m propagation position, keeping the same sample and illumination.

  1. Treat the two detector positions as alternative acquisitions, not simultaneous beam splitting.
  2. Compare an edge fringe with a quantitative phase map; retrieval assumptions must be supplied.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Coherent 20 keV input — Spatial coherence supports observable Fresnel contrast.
  2. Weakly absorbing sample (Sample) — Both phase and absorption alter the transmitted field.
  3. Near-contact image (Photodetector) — Short propagation emphasizes absorption contrast.
  4. 0.5 m free-space path — Propagation converts some phase structure to intensity modulation.
  5. Propagated image (Photodetector) — Edge fringes depend on distance, coherence and detector resolution.
  6. Model-based phase retrieval — Retrieval requires explicit assumptions and calibrated geometry.

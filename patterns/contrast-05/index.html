Differential interference contrast

The example

A 550 nm polarized field is split into neighboring orthogonal-polarization paths with an illustrative 0.3 µm specimen shear, then recombined with adjustable bias before an analyzer.

  1. Rotate the conceptual shear direction relative to a cell edge and compare which gradient is sampled.
  2. Change the phase bias around quadrature and distinguish contrast polarity from physical height.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Input polarizer (Polarizer) — Prepares polarization at 45° to the prism axes.
  2. First Nomarski prism — Creates the two orthogonally polarized probes.
  3. Cell point A (Sample) — First neighboring optical path through the cell.
  4. Cell point B (Sample) — Second path, displaced along the chosen shear direction.
  5. Second prism + bias — Recombines paths with a chosen quarter-wave bias.
  6. Analyzer / camera (Polarizer) — Converts relative phase into detected intensity.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Polarizers and matched shearing prisms around the specimen imaging system.

Key constraints

Contrast depends on shear direction and bias; birefringence can complicate interpretation.

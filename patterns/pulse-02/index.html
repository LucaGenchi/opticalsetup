The example

An 800 nm, 100 fs pulse passes a double-pass prism pair before glass-heavy microscope optics. Prism separation and insertion tune dispersion; a sample-plane diagnostic verifies compensation.

  1. Identify which distances and glass insertions tune the spectral path.
  2. Add glass after the compressor conceptually and explain why an upstream measurement can miss residual chirp.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 800 nm pulse (Pulsed Laser) — Bandwidth and chirp define the compensation need.
  2. First prism (Prism) — Angularly disperses the spectrum.
  3. Second prism (Prism) — Separation and insertion control spectral optical paths.
  4. Return mirror (Retroreflector) — Double pass recombines the dispersed wavelengths.
  5. Objective / glass path (Objective) — Adds material dispersion after compensation.
  6. Sample-plane diagnostic — Compensation should be checked where the experiment occurs.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Prism pairs, grating arrangements, chirped mirrors, or guided dispersive stages.

Key constraints

Higher-order dispersion, bandwidth, losses, and spatial chirp can remain after GDD compensation.

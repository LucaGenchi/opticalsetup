The example

An 80 MHz oscillator crosses a synchronized Pockels cell between polarizers. Passing one of every 80 pulses gives a nominal 1 MHz output; another programmed gate can select a short burst.

  1. Check the one-in-80 arithmetic and distinguish selection rate from pulse duration.
  2. Select four consecutive pulses conceptually and note their unchanged 12.5 ns internal spacing.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 80 MHz oscillator (Pulsed Laser) — Input pulse spacing is 12.5 ns.
  2. Input polarization (Polarizer) — Prepares the state accepted by the switching system.
  3. Pockels-cell gate (EOM) — Synchronized voltage selects pulses by polarization rotation.
  4. Output polarization split (Polarizing BS) — Passes selected pulses and rejects others.
  5. Selected 1 MHz train — One-in-80 selection is the nominal teaching schedule.
  6. Rejected 79 pulses (Beam dump) — Unused pulse energy goes to a safe dump.
  7. Divider / burst timing — Phase and gate width must match the oscillator clock.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Synchronized electro-optic or acousto-optic gate with output selection.

Key constraints

Switch speed, extinction, jitter, and pulse-energy redistribution matter.

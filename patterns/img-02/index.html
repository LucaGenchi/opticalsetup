Patterns / Transferring images, beams, and pupils

IMG-02 · Pattern family

Afocal telescope / beam expander

Change a collimated beam's diameter and angular spread.

Ray exampleThreefold Keplerian beam expander

532 nm input — A collimated beam with 3 mm diameter.

Open in full canvas ↗Download setup

Ray model: This live example uses paraxial thin lenses; diffraction, real-lens aberrations and damage at the focus are absent.

The example

A 50 mm lens and a 150 mm lens, separated by 200 mm, expand a collimated 3 mm beam to approximately 9 mm.

  1. Open the ray example and compare beam width before and after the telescope.
  2. Move L2 away from the 200 mm lens separation to see residual convergence or divergence.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 532 nm input (CW Laser) — A collimated beam with 3 mm diameter.
  2. L1 · 50 mm (Thin convex lens) — Produces the intermediate real focus.
  3. Real focus — 50 mm behind the first lens; keep high-power objects away.
  4. L2 · 150 mm (Thin convex lens) — Recollimates after the shared focus.
  5. Expanded beam (Camera) — Inspect increased beam width, not a magnified target.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Keplerian, Galilean, or reflective telescope with coordinated focal lengths.

Key constraints

Expansion trades beam diameter against divergence; intermediate foci and clipping matter.

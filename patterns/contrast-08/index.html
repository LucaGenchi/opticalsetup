Excitation plus depletion

The example

An illustrative 640 nm excitation spot and 775 nm vortex-shaped depletion beam share an objective. Depletion timing and the central intensity minimum are adjusted before fluorescence reaches a confocal detector.

  1. Identify both spatial registration and pulse-order requirements.
  2. Shift the depletion minimum off the excitation maximum conceptually and explain loss of useful fluorescence.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 640 nm excitation (Pulsed Laser) — Excites the fluorophore before depletion.
  2. 775 nm depletion (Pulsed Laser) — Provides the later stimulated-emission pulse.
  3. Vortex phase mask (Phase object) — Creates the desired depletion minimum; polarization also matters.
  4. Dichroic combiner (Dichroic mirror) — Registers both colors on a shared optical axis.
  5. Shared objective (Objective) — Overlaps the excitation maximum and depletion minimum.
  6. Fluorescent target (Sample) — Only the undepleted region contributes desired fluorescence.
  7. Confocal detector (Photomultiplier (PMT)) — Filters emission and selects the conjugate focal region.

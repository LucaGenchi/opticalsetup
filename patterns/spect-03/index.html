Image slicing / integral-field feed

Rearrange a two-dimensional field into inputs suitable for spectroscopy.

Schematic exampleThree-slice integral-field spectrograph feed

The example

Divide a small square sky field into three strips, relay them into a pseudo-slit and disperse them together; retain a lookup from slit position back to each strip.

  1. Track one location through its slice, pseudo-slit segment and detector spectrum.
  2. Check that the slice lookup is retained; a summed spectrum cannot reconstruct the original field.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 2D sky field — Three equal strips define this teaching field.
  2. Slice 1 — First field strip is redirected to one pseudo-slit segment.
  3. Slice 2 — Central strip retains its distinct spatial coordinate.
  4. Slice 3 — Third strip is redirected to the last segment.
  5. Reformatted pseudo-slit (Slit) — Segments are end-to-end, not optically summed.
  6. Dispersing relay (Diffraction grating) — Forms a spectrum for every slit position.
  7. Spatial × spectral camera (Camera) — A reconstruction table returns a data cube.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Image slicers, lenslet arrays, or fiber feeds forming spectrograph inputs.

Key constraints

Spatial sampling, slit packing, losses, and crosstalk compete for detector area.

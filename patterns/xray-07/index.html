The example

An illustrative source grating G₀, phase grating G₁ and analyzer grating G₂ measure an X-ray specimen. Translate G₂ through five phase steps to estimate absorption, differential phase and dark-field channels.

  1. Identify the source, phase and analyzer grating roles instead of treating all three as dispersers.
  2. Compare sample and reference stepping curves; visibility loss is distinct from mean attenuation.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Extended X-ray source — Source coherence is conditioned by G₀.
  2. G₀ source grating — Creates an array of mutually incoherent sourcelets.
  3. Specimen (Sample) — Changes transmission, fringe shift and visibility.
  4. G₁ phase grating — Creates the near-field interference structure.
  5. G₂ stepped analyzer — Five translations sample the local fringe phase.
  6. Integrating detector (Photodetector) — Resolves the stepping curve per pixel.
  7. Three-channel fit — Separates mean, phase and visibility relative to flat-field steps.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Talbot or Talbot-Lau arrangements with phase/analyzer gratings and optional source grating.

Key constraints

Grating geometry, energy bandwidth, alignment, and stepping calibration limit sensitivity.

Evanescent-field excitation

The example

A 488 nm beam enters a glass prism and meets water at a chosen 70° internal angle (n≈1.52 and 1.33). Fluorescence near the interface is collected from below, separate from the reflected beam.

  1. Compare the chosen angle with arcsin(1.33/1.52), about 61°.
  2. Decrease incidence toward the critical angle and discuss penetration depth and unwanted propagating excitation.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 488 nm excitation (CW Laser) — Collimated illumination enters the prism.
  2. Glass prism (Prism) — Sets an internal angle above the glass/water critical angle.
  3. Glass / water interface (Sample) — A decaying evanescent field excites nearby fluorophores.
  4. Reflected beam dump (Beam dump) — Receives the totally internally reflected pump.
  5. Below-surface objective (Objective) — Collects fluorescence independently of excitation.
  6. Filtered fluorescence (Camera) — An emission filter rejects residual 488 nm light.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Prism-based or high-NA objective-based total-internal-reflection illumination.

Key constraints

Incidence angle, indices, and sample scattering determine the excitation field.

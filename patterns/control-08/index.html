Pupil and image diagnostic branches

The example

A post-objective pickoff sends one branch to an image-conjugate camera and another through a Bertrand lens to a pupil-conjugate camera.

  1. Use a specimen grid to identify the image plane and an aperture stop to identify the pupil plane.
  2. Compare field clipping with pupil decentering in the separate diagnostics.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Test specimen (Sample) — Use a grid or bead for image alignment.
  2. Objective (Objective) — Defines image and aperture conjugates.
  3. Diagnostic pickoff (Beamsplitter) — Main scientific channel remains available.
  4. Main image camera (Camera) — Primary imaging output.
  5. Image diagnostic (Camera) — Inspect field focus and vignetting.
  6. Bertrand lens (Thin convex lens) — Reimage the back aperture onto a sensor.
  7. Pupil diagnostic (Camera) — Inspect aperture fill, centering and obscuration.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Switchable or simultaneous cameras/eyepieces conjugated to image and pupil planes.

Key constraints

Probe branches must represent the working path without creating misleading conjugates.

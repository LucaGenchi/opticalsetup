Atmospheric dispersion compensation

Introduce angular dispersion opposing that accumulated in the atmosphere.

The example

A teaching 400–800 nm stellar field at 60° zenith distance crosses two counter-rotating prism assemblies. Their combined angular dispersion opposes the atmosphere before a slit or fiber focus.

  1. Compare the zero-dispersion setting near zenith with the chosen off-zenith setting.
  2. Check residual blue/red separation at the slit; canceling mean deviation is insufficient.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Dispersed stellar field — Atmospheric refraction separates wavelength-dependent directions.
  2. Prism assembly A (Prism) — Dispersion vector rotates with the first assembly.
  3. Prism assembly B (Prism) — Counter-rotation changes net dispersion while managing beam pointing.
  4. Science focusing optic (Thin convex lens) — Maps residual color spread onto the entrance aperture.
  5. Spectrometer entrance (Slit) — Broadband coupling requires low residual color separation.
  6. Zenith-angle setting — Chosen observing geometry sets the required prism rotation.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Counter-rotating dispersive prism assemblies or equivalent compensators.

Key constraints

Correction depends on observing geometry, wavelength range, and residual aberrations.

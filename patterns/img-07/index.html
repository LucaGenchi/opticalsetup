IMG-07 · Optical layout

Scheimpflug imaging

Bring a tilted object plane into focus with coordinated optical and sensor tilts.

Schematic exampleTilted-plane inspection camera

Tilted specimen — Object plane tilted 30° relative to the nominal sensor plane.

Open in full canvas ↗Download setup

Diagram scope: The plane intersection is schematic and does not solve lens tilt, projective distortion, depth of field or off-axis aberrations. The editable diagram contains annotations and connection arrows; it does not run an optical simulation.

The example

A 30° tilted specimen is observed through a tilted lens and sensor arranged so their plane extensions meet along a common line.

  1. Follow all three plane-extension lines to the common intersection.
  2. Treat sensor tilt and longitudinal focus as separate adjustments.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Tilted specimen — Object plane tilted 30° relative to the nominal sensor plane.
  2. Tiltable lens (Thin convex lens) — Lens plane extension must share the Scheimpflug intersection.
  3. Tilted sensor (Camera) — Tilt independently from focusing translation.
  4. Common intersection — Plane extensions meet here; this is a geometrical constraint.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Tilted object, lens principal plane, and image plane with compatible conjugates.

Key constraints

The plane condition does not by itself guarantee acceptable depth of field or aberrations.

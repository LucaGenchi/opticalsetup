COMPUTE-04 · Measurement architecture

Coded-aperture imaging

Encode a scene with a known mask and recover it using a measurement model.

Schematic exampleBinary coded-mask shadow camera

Distant scene — Unknown angular brightness distribution.

Diagram scope: Mask coding, angular response, multiplex noise and image inversion are not executed. The editable diagram contains annotations and connection arrows; it does not run an optical simulation.

The example

A 31×31 binary mask sits 20 mm before a detector; point-source calibration supplies the decoding response and detector background.

  1. Distinguish the physical mask from the computational decoder.
  2. Compare a known point response with an extended scene before assuming ideal decoding.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Distant scene — Unknown angular brightness distribution.
  2. 31×31 mask — Known opaque/open binary pattern.
  3. Shadow detector (Camera) — Record the superposed mask shadows.
  4. Correlation decoder — Compare data with calibrated shifted mask responses.
  5. Recovered directions — Intended angular image product.
  6. Point calibration (Point source) — Measure the instrument response at known angles.
  7. Dark exposure — Record detector background with input blocked.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Key constraints

Conditioning, noise, model error, and scene assumptions limit recovery.

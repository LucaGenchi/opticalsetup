FIELD-05 · Optical layout

Balanced detection

Subtract complementary optical outputs to reject common-mode fluctuations.

Schematic exampleDifferential balanced photodetection

Optical port A — Nominal 0.5 mW complementary port.

The example

Two complementary optical outputs with nominal 0.5 mW each enter matched 0.8 A/W photodiodes; a difference amplifier suppresses their common term.

  1. Calculate each nominal photocurrent as 0.4 mA.
  2. Introduce a 1% responsivity mismatch and identify the residual common-mode term.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Optical port A — Nominal 0.5 mW complementary port.
  2. Optical port B — Nominal 0.5 mW complementary port.
  3. Photodiode A (Photodetector) — Chosen responsivity 0.8 A/W.
  4. Photodiode B (Photodetector) — Match responsivity, gain and delay to A.
  5. Difference amplifier — Subtract photocurrents: iA − iB.
  6. Differential output — Equal nominal inputs give zero DC difference.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Two matched photodetectors and differential electrical readout.

Key constraints

Optical balance, detector linearity, and bandwidth limit common-mode rejection.

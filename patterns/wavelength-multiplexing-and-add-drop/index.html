Wavelength multiplexing and add/drop

Insert, route, or remove wavelength channels from a shared optical connection.

Open setup in canvas ↗Download setup

Model limits. This is a native free-space wavelength-routing equivalent, not a microring or fiber network. Ideal dichroic bands omit guided coupling, insertion-loss spectra, linewidth, channel crosstalk and encoded data.

The setup

Three spatially separated lasers at 1530, 1550 and 1570 nm enter a common free-space bus through two wavelength-selective combining dichroics. A 1550 nm band-reflecting dichroic drops the middle wavelength; a second band reflector adds a separate 1550 nm source.

  1. Inspect the drop detector: its only incident wavelength is 1550 nm. The output spectrometer receives the two outer carriers plus the replacement.
  2. Move the first reflector center to 1500 nm to demonstrate the missed-channel boundary; the original 1550 nm channel then reaches the add reflector and is diverted into its rejection detector.

Components in this setup

  • CW Laser
  • Dichroic mirror
  • Photodetector
  • Spectrometer

Arrangement and variants

Filter banks, arrayed-waveguide gratings, or coupled-ring add/drop networks.

Design constraints

Channel spacing, crosstalk, loss, and thermal drift constrain operation.

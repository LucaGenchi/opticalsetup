Light-sheet illumination

Restrict excitation to a thin plane within the observed volume.

The example

A cylindrical lens forms a thin illumination sheet through the specimen; a separate objective collects fluorescence at 90°.

  1. Follow orthogonal illumination and detection axes.
  2. Compare changing sheet thickness with stepping the specimen through the fixed detection plane.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 488 nm beam (CW Laser) — Nominal 2 mm beam before the cylindrical optics.
  2. Sheet-forming cylinder — Focus one axis and leave the other extended.
  3. Illuminated slice (Sample) — Sheet lies in the detection focal plane.
  4. Detection objective (Objective) — Collects orthogonal to illumination.
  5. Slice camera (Camera) — Image plane captures one optical section.
  6. Specimen translation — Step through a volume with separate acquisitions.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Cylindrical focusing or a scanned beam; orthogonal or oblique detection arrangements.

Key constraints

Sheet thickness, useful propagation length, and access geometry trade against each other.

References

Sources for the arrangement and its principles.

What follows

Fluorescence collection along the detection axis; the illumination path ends beyond the sheet.

Wavelength multiplexing and add/drop

Insert, route, or remove wavelength channels from a shared optical connection.

The example

Three carriers at 1530, 1550 and 1570 nm share a bus; the 1550 nm channel is removed and replaced at one intermediate station.

  1. Follow the dropped wavelength independently of the through channels.
  2. Change the add wavelength and explain why an occupied channel would create interference.

Follow the arrangement

  1. 1530 / 1570 nm (CW Laser) — Two through channels enter the multiplexer.
  2. Input multiplexer — Combine channels into a single guided bus.
  3. 1550 nm drop — A wavelength-selective port removes only the middle channel.
  4. 1550 nm add — Inject a replacement transmitter on the freed channel.
  5. Through receiver (Photodetector) — Receive the restored three-channel bus.
  6. 1550 nm input (CW Laser) — The original middle channel enters separately.
  7. Drop receiver (Photodetector) — Receive the removed data channel.
  8. Replacement 1550 nm (CW Laser) — Independent transmitter injected at the add port.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Filter banks, arrayed-waveguide gratings, or coupled-ring add/drop networks.

Key constraints

Channel spacing, crosstalk, loss, and thermal drift constrain operation.

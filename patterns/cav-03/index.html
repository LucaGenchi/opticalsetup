Power and signal recycling

The example

A Michelson operated near a dark readout port places a power-recycling mirror at the bright input and a signal-recycling mirror at the output. Both mirrors create additional resonant paths.

  1. Trace the input recycling loop separately from the output recycling loop.
  2. Discuss how changing signal-recycling detuning can alter bandwidth without simply increasing all signals.

Follow the arrangement

  1. Laser input (CW Laser) — Feeds the bright port.
  2. Power-recycling mirror (Mirror) — Returns light leaving the bright port to the splitter.
  3. Michelson splitter (Beamsplitter) — Defines bright and dark port combinations.
  4. Arm A cavity — Round-trip phase contributes to differential readout.
  5. Arm B cavity — Second arm must share the chosen operating point.
  6. Signal-recycling mirror (Mirror) — Tunes the output-port resonant response.
  7. Output readout (Photodetector) — Receives the extracted signal sidebands.

Design choices

Arrangement and variants

Recycling mirrors form coupled cavities around selected interferometer ports.

Key constraints

Coupled resonances and control interactions complicate bandwidth and noise optimization.

Faraday mirror

A 45° Faraday rotator in front of a mirror returns light at 90° to how it left, whatever an unknown birefringent element did to it on the way out and back.

Background

Light sent down a fibre and reflected back at the far end comes back in a polarization nobody chose: the fibre's birefringence changes with temperature, stress and bending, so the state that returns drifts. A Faraday mirror removes the drift. It is a Faraday rotator that turns the polarization by 45° on each pass, followed by a mirror[1].

The trick is that the Faraday effect is not reciprocal. A waveplate crossed backwards undoes what it did on the way out, so a reciprocal element followed by a mirror and the same element again returns light in a state that depends on that element. A Faraday rotator turns the polarization in a sense fixed by its magnetic field, so the return pass adds a second 45° instead of taking the first away. The 90° in total turns the light orthogonal, and the birefringent element, crossed again on the way back, cannot undo that: whatever it is, the light comes back at 90° to how it entered[1]. Interferometric fibre sensors and fibre lasers use it to make the round trip insensitive to the fibre's state.

What this setup demonstrates

A horizontally polarized 1064 nm laser of 10 mW passes a polarizing beamsplitter, a quarter-wave plate at 20° standing in for an unknown birefringence, a Faraday rotator of TGG and a mirror. The rotator is 20.94 mm long in a 1 T field, a turn of 45° per pass at 1064 nm; its inspector gives the turn in each direction. The light comes back horizontal-turned-vertical, and the PBS reflects all of it to the power meter, which reads 10.0 mW.

Things to try. Each starts from the setup as it opens:

One changePower meter
Turn the quarter-wave plate to any angle, 65° say10.0 mW
Field of the rotator to 0 T: the plate alone, crossed twice, acts as a half-wave plate at 20°4.13 mW
Field to 0 T and the plate to 45°: the ordinary send–return trick, which works only at this angle10.0 mW
Rotator length to 10.47 mm, 22.5° per pass7.07 mW

The setup is the polarization send–return separation with the quarter-wave plate's job taken over by the rotator: there the plate must sit at exactly 45°, here it may sit anywhere.

What you won't see

The fibre is represented by a quarter-wave plate: the fiber element passes polarization unchanged, so a waveplate at an arbitrary angle stands in for the birefringence of a real fibre. The rotator is a thin element with a Verdet constant measured at room temperature (TGG, one sample); its index, path delay and absorption, and the mirror's own phase between s and p, are not modelled, and the PBS has perfect extinction. The light that returns through the PBS towards the laser simply ends there.

Fresnel rhomb as an achromatic quarter-wave retarder

Two total internal reflections inside a glass rhomb shift the phase of s against p by a quarter wave, nearly the same from blue to infrared, so linear light at 45° leaves circular.

Open in the canvas →

A live trace of the setup above, shown as a picture. Open it in the canvas to take it apart, retune it, and save or export it as your own.

Background

Total internal reflection returns all of the light, but not with the same phase for the two polarizations: s and p come back shifted against each other by an amount that depends on the angle of incidence and on the index of the glass[1]. Fresnel found that two such reflections at the right angle add up to a quarter wave, and built a rhomb of glass that makes circular light from linear light at 45°[2].

A waveplate gets its quarter wave from a thickness of birefringent crystal, which is exactly a quarter wave only at its design wavelength. The rhomb gets it from the index of glass, which changes little across the visible and near infrared, so the rhomb is nearly achromatic[2]. (The app’s own quarter-wave plate is ideal: a quarter wave at every wavelength.)

What this setup demonstrates

Each rhomb is freeform glass of N-BK7, a parallelogram with an acute angle of 54.6°. The beam enters and leaves through its end faces at normal incidence and is totally reflected twice in between, at 54.6°.

With its surfaces set to Bare glass (Fresnel), each reflection shifts s against p by 45.8° at 450 nm and by 44.8° at 1064 nm. Laser light polarized at 45° leaves left circular at both wavelengths: the polarimeter in the inspector reads S₃/S₀ = −1.00, and S₂/S₀ = −0.03 at 450 nm and +0.01 at 1064 nm, the small remainder of a retardance of 91.6° and 89.7° instead of 90°. The two end faces each reflect 4 % of the light, and the polarimeter reads 915 µW and 920 µW.

The third rhomb keeps the default Per-surface transmission. It reflects as an ideal mirror, with no phase between s and p, and the light leaves linear at 45°.

Things to try, each a single change from the setup as it opens:

What you won't see

The glass is lossless and uncoated. The end faces are met at normal incidence, where they change no polarization; the part of the light they reflect is not drawn. Every ray and face normal lies in the drawing plane, so s is out of the drawing at every face.

Brewster window

At Brewster’s angle a glass surface reflects none of the light polarized in the plane of incidence: a plate turned to that angle passes p light whole and takes part of s, and a pile of such plates polarizes unpolarized light.

Background

Light meeting a glass surface is partly reflected, by an amount that depends on the angle and on the polarization. For light polarized in the plane of incidence, p, the reflection vanishes at one angle, Brewster’s angle, where tan θB = n2/n1[1]: 56.3° from air into glass of index 1.5. Light polarized across that plane, s, is still partly reflected there.

A plate of glass turned to Brewster’s angle therefore passes p light without loss at either face. Laser tubes are closed with such windows for that reason[1]. Unpolarized light loses part of its s half at every face, so a stack of plates at Brewster’s angle, a pile of plates, leaves it more and more polarized along p[2].

What this setup demonstrates

Each plate is freeform glass of index 1.5 with its surfaces set to Bare glass (Fresnel), so each face transmits s and p by the Fresnel equations at the angle the ray actually meets it. The plates are turned to 56.3°.

In the first row a laser polarized at 0°, p in the drawing, passes one plate and the power meter reads 1.00 mW: nothing is lost. In the second row the laser is polarized at 90°, s, and the meter reads 726 µW: each face reflects about 15 % of it. In the third row an unpolarized LED passes four plates. The polarimeter reads 639 µW, partly linear 0°, with a degree of polarization of 57 %: all of the p half arrives, and 28 % of the s half.

Things to try, each a single change from the setup as it opens:

ChangeReading
Both single plates turned to 30°p 950 µW, s 888 µW: away from Brewster’s angle p is reflected too
Both single plates set to Per-surface transmissionp and s both 960 µW (98 % a face, the same for both)
Two of the four plates removedthe polarimeter reads 764 µW and a degree of polarization of 31 %
What you won't see

Only the light that passes each face is followed: the part a face reflects is not drawn, and reflections between the faces of a plate (ghosts) and their interference are not modelled. The plates are lossless and uncoated, with one index at every wavelength.

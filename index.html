Draw the optical setup. Trace it, measure it, share it.

A free, browser-based 2D optical workbench. Place lasers, lenses, mirrors, fibers and detectors on a virtual table: the beams trace as you move things, the detectors read power, spectrum, polarization and pulse duration, and the whole setup travels as a link or a paper-ready figure. No install, no account, nothing stored on a server.

Open the canvas →

Live ray tracing

Trace reflection and refraction on supported 2D optical surfaces, including spherical, conic and aspheric profiles, recomputed every time a component moves. Catalogue glass models add wavelength-dependent refraction and dispersion; paraxial and other approximations are described in the wiki.

A real element library

OpticalSetup covers most of the hardware you'd find on an actual optics bench — sources, optics, and detectors alike — and the library keeps growing as new elements are added.

Direct manipulation

Select a component to get resize handles, a rotation handle, and a tuning knob for its key parameter — right on the canvas, no dialog required.

Paper-ready export

Export a clean crop of your setup as SVG or PNG, ready to drop straight into a manuscript or slide deck. Frame exactly what you want with an optional Figure frame — it defines the export bounds without ever showing up in the artwork.

Share a link, not a file

The Share action compresses your sketch into the URL itself and generates a QR code. Opening the link rebuilds the exact setup — no account, no server-side scene storage.

Readouts with stated limits

Inspect the signal, spectrum, polarization and pulse timing computed for your setup. Selected calculations — catalogue glass dispersion, pulse duration after dispersion, hollow-core fiber spectra, polarization through waveplates — are checked against independent numerical references; the validation table lists the covered cases and tolerances. Component pages explain the model assumptions and limitations.

How it works

  1. Choose a source from the component library — a laser, LED, or broadband point source.
  2. Place optics in the beam: lenses, mirrors, filters, splitters, anything the light should pass through.
  3. Add a detector and select it to read the power, spectrum, polarization or pulse duration it receives.

OpticalSetup computes what it shows: ray paths from the surfaces you draw, dispersion from catalogue glass data, and detector readings from the light that actually arrives, each within limits stated on the component's wiki page. It is built for designing, understanding and communicating setups; for tolerancing, full lens optimization or 3D analysis, use dedicated optical design software.

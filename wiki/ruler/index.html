Ruler

Measures a distance or an angle on the bench and labels it, with Markdown and LaTeX in the label.

Open in the canvas →

A live trace, not a picture of one — but this preview is not interactive. Open it in the canvas to move things, change parameters, and save or export your own version.

In the real world

Figures of optical setups state the distances and angles that matter: a lens placed one focal length from a pinhole, the separation of a 4f relay, the fold angle at a mirror, the crossing angle of two beams in a crystal. A dimension line with its value, or an arc between two lines with the angle, says it on the figure itself.

In OpticalSetup

Choose whether the ruler measures a distance or an angle, then select it. A distance ruler has a round handle at each end: drag one to move that end. An angle ruler has one at the vertex and one at the tip of each arm. Dragging an arm's tip turns only that arm about the vertex, smoothly and without the grid, keeping its length, which the inspector sets; the other arm stays where it is. The purple knob tunes the angle by itself, and the vertex handle moves the whole ruler. Dragging the line or the label also moves the whole ruler, and the inspector sets every length and the angle by number.

With auto clamp, on by default, a handle clicks into place near what a measurement usually starts from. A distance end or the vertex lands on a component's centre or its optically active point (a laser's aperture, an objective's tip), on another ruler's handle, on a bend in a beam such as its fold at a mirror, or on a beam's axis. Away from these it falls back to the grid when Snap is on. An arm clicks onto a beam whose axis runs through the vertex, or turns toward a component, and a dashed purple line shows what it has clicked onto. The beam axis is the central ray of each traced beam (for an object's ray fan, the ray along the object's axis), or an arrow drawn as a beam; a point source has no single axis, so only its centre is a target. Hold Alt to place or turn freely, or Shift to keep a distance to 45° steps or the angle to 5° steps.

The distance is in bench millimetres, the coordinates the tracer computes in; the angle between the arms runs from 0 to 180°, its sign only saying which way the second arm opens. The label is Markdown, as in a text label, with LaTeX formulas between dollar signs, and {value} in it is replaced by the measurement with the chosen number of decimal places: $f = {value}$ reads f = 100.0 mm, with the unit set upright inside the formula, and a label without {value}, such as $2f$, shows no number at all. A distance label runs along the line, above or below it, and turns so it never reads upside down; an angle label stays level, out along the bisector. The dimension line can carry arrowheads and graduation ticks at a 1-2-5 step.

The example reads a lens's focal length off the bench, from the lens to where the beam crosses, and the fold angle at a mirror.

Simplified vs. reality

The ruler measures the drawing: the straight distance between its two handles, or the angle between its arms, not an optical path length, which would also depend on the indices of the media between them. It does not follow a beam or attach to a component: a handle clamped to a part, or an arm laid along a beam, stays where it was put when the part or the beam moves. The ruler is diagram-only and never absorbs or redirects rays.

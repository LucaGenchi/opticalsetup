Sample

Represents an illuminated specimen with configurable transmission and signal channels, for exploring excitation delivery, fluorescence collection, and qualitative nonlinear optical emission.

Open in the canvas →

A live trace, not a picture of one — but this preview is not interactive. Open it in the canvas to move things, change parameters, and save or export your own version.

In the real world

A specimen can transmit or absorb excitation light and generate an optical signal. Fluorescence and coherent nonlinear signals arise through different processes; nonlinear microscopy includes two-photon fluorescence, second-harmonic generation, and coherent anti-Stokes Raman scattering[1].

In OpticalSetup

Orient the sample across the beam: at zero rotation its long axis is horizontal, so a horizontal incoming beam needs a 90° sample rotation. Choose the specimen mode, excitation transmission, and desired channels. The model supports stacked fluorescence, SHG, THG, SFG, and CARS signals; SFG and CARS require two different excitation wavelengths meeting at the same spot.

The example produces a fluorescent signal under 488 nm illumination. Adjust its emission and transmission, then use a filter and detector to distinguish emitted light from the excitation. Parametric channels use a forward lobe with an optional weaker backward contribution.

Simplified vs. reality

Signals are bounded qualitative proxies, not measured cross-sections or calibrated conversion efficiencies. The model does not reconstruct a three-dimensional specimen or predict photochemistry, bleaching, or a diffraction-limited point-spread function.

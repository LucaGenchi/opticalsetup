Nonlinear frequency conversion

Find the generated wavelength of second- and third-harmonic generation, sum- and difference-frequency generation, the signal and idler of an optical parametric oscillator, or the CARS and CSRS lines of a pump and Stokes pair. Every wave is shown as a vacuum wavelength, frequency and photon energy.

    Model
    Photon-energy conservation for χ⁽²⁾ and χ⁽³⁾ processes. No crystal, phase-matching or efficiency model.
    Evidence
    Equations checked against independent Python references, with published or exact algebraic anchors. These checks do not establish experimental accuracy.
    Example
    1064 nm → 532 nm (SHG, as in NIST’s example [2]) and 354.67 nm (THG); a 1030 nm-pumped OPO with a 1550 nm signal has a 3070.2 nm idler.

    Energy conservation

    In a parametric process the medium returns to its initial state, so the photon energies balance and the generated frequency follows from the input frequencies [1]. With vacuum wavelengths λ = c/ν:

    SHG: ν2ω=2ν,THG: ν3ω=3ν,λnω=λ/n\text{SHG: }\nu_{2\omega}=2\nu,\qquad \text{THG: }\nu_{3\omega}=3\nu,\qquad \lambda_{n\omega}=\lambda/nSHG: ν2ω​=2ν,THG: ν3ω​=3ν,λnω​=λ/n

    SHG is the degenerate case of SFG, and DFG is the process behind parametric amplification: the lower-frequency input grows as the difference frequency is generated. In an OPO the signal is, by convention, the higher-frequency output; signal and idler meet at the degeneracy wavelength 2λp. One pump photon yields one signal and one idler photon (Manley–Rowe), so at most a fraction λp/λ of the converted pump power can go into each output [1]. THG is generated either directly through χ⁽³⁾ or by cascading SHG with SFG [1]. CARS and CSRS are four-wave mixing in χ⁽³⁾: a pump and a Stokes beam drive a vibration at their difference frequency, and the pump and Stokes photons scatter off it on either side. This page gives their wavelengths only; the Coherent Raman scattering calculator also solves from a Raman shift, estimates the spectral resolution and shows the probed window on reference spectra.

    Precision and limits

    Known wavelengths are accepted from 100 to 20000 nm; generated waves may fall outside that interval (DFG of close wavelengths reaches the terahertz range). DFG needs two different inputs; an OPO output must be longer than the pump. An independent reference computes each process in SI frequencies and checks the page to 10⁻⁹ relative, with exact rational anchors.

    Energy conservation says which wavelength a process would generate, not whether it does: phase matching, crystal transparency and damage, acceptance bandwidths, pulse overlap, power and conversion efficiency are not modelled. Wavelengths inside a medium are shorter by its refractive index; this page reports vacuum wavelengths throughout.

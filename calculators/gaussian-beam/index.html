Gaussian beam

Give a laser beam by its waist, divergence or Rayleigh range, and read its size, wavefront curvature and Gouy phase at any distance. Add a thin lens to see where the beam focuses and how small the spot is.

    Model
    Paraxial Gaussian beam (TEM₀₀, or the embedded Gaussian of an M² beam) in a uniform medium; an ideal thin lens by Self’s equations.
    Evidence
    Checked against an independent Python reference that uses a different method: ABCD propagation of the complex beam parameter. These checks do not establish experimental accuracy.

    The Gaussian beam

    Most lasers emit, or can be cleaned up to, a beam whose intensity falls off from its axis as a Gaussian. Such a beam keeps its Gaussian shape as it travels: only its width and the curvature of its wavefront change [1][3]. Its width is given as the radius w where the intensity has fallen to 1/e² (13.5 %) of the peak; 86.5 % of the power lies inside it.

    w₀ is the waist, the narrowest radius; z is the distance from it; λ the vacuum wavelength and n the index of the medium.

    The Rayleigh range zR divides the near field from the far field. Within ±zR of the waist the beam stays nearly parallel: at zR its radius has grown by √2 and its area doubled. Far beyond, the radius grows linearly with distance, at the divergence angle θ. The wavefront is flat at the waist, most curved at z = zR, and flat again far away, where it looks like a spherical wave from the waist. The Gouy phase ψ is an extra phase the beam picks up through its focus: 90° from the waist to the far field, 180° from far before to far after.

    Waist and divergence trade against each other: their product w₀θ = λ/(πn), the beam parameter product, is fixed by the wavelength. A beam twice as wide spreads half as fast. With the page’s example, a 1064 nm beam with a 0.5 mm waist, the Rayleigh range is 738 mm and the half-angle divergence 0.677 mrad; 1 m from the waist the radius is 0.842 mm and the wavefront’s radius of curvature 1.54 m. At 1 W its peak intensity is 255 W/cm² at the waist and 89.8 W/cm² there.

    Beam quality M²

    Real beams spread faster than an ideal Gaussian of the same waist. The beam propagation ratio M² measures by how much: it is the beam parameter product relative to the ideal one, measured from second-moment widths [4]. Such a beam still follows the Gaussian formulas with λ replaced by M²λ, the embedded Gaussian rule [2][4]:

    zR=πw02nM2λ,θ=M2λπnw0,w0θ=M2λπnz_R = \frac{\pi w_0^2 n}{M^2\lambda},\qquad \theta = \frac{M^2\lambda}{\pi n w_0},\qquad w_0\theta = \frac{M^2\lambda}{\pi n}zR​=M2λπw02​n​,θ=πnw0​M2λ​,w0​θ=πnM2λ​

    So the 0.5 mm, 1064 nm beam with M² = 1.5 has a Rayleigh range of 492 mm instead of 738 mm, and diverges at 1.02 mrad. M² describes widths and divergence; it says nothing about the shape of the profile, so for M² > 1 the page gives no peak intensity, half-maximum diameter or Gouy phase.

    Focusing with a thin lens

    A thin lens turns one Gaussian beam into another. Self’s equations give the new waist and where it forms, for an input waist a distance s before a lens of focal length f [2]:

    w0′=w0(1−s/f)2+(zR/f)2,s′′=f+(s−f) f2(s−f)2+zR2w_0' = \frac{w_0}{\sqrt{\left(1-s/f\right)^2+\left(z_R/f\right)^2}},\qquad s'' = f + \frac{(s-f)\,f^2}{(s-f)^2+z_R^2}w0′​=(1−s/f)2+(zR​/f)2​w0​​,s′′=f+(s−f)2+zR2​(s−f)f2​
    s″ is the distance from the lens to the new waist; negative means a virtual waist before the lens.

    Two limits are worth knowing. A beam that reaches the lens collimated (its waist at the lens, zR ≫ f) focuses to w₀′ ≈ λf/(πw₀) at almost exactly f. A 1064 nm beam with a 1 mm waist and a 100 mm lens gives a 33.8 µm waist at 99.89 mm. But when zR is not much longer than f, the waist forms well short of the focal point: with a 0.25 mm waist (zR = 185 mm) the same lens focuses at 77.3 mm. Ray optics would put both at 100 mm. A waist placed at the front focal plane (s = f) comes out at the back focal plane, magnified by f/zR: a 50 µm HeNe waist becomes 403 µm.

    Precision and limits

    An independent Python reference propagates the complex beam parameter q with ABCD matrices, finds the waist after a lens where q is imaginary, and recovers a waist from a divergence by bisection on the far-field limit. It never uses the closed forms above. The page agrees with it to 10⁻⁹ relative, through converging, imaging, virtual-waist and diverging lenses.

    The formulas are paraxial: they hold while the beam’s angles are small, roughly below 0.1 rad, so a waist of a few wavelengths or less is outside them. The page refuses half-angle divergences above 0.3 rad and warns above 0.1 rad after a lens. The lens is ideal and thin, with no aberrations or clipping. An aperture of radius a passes 1 − exp(−2a²/w²) of a TEM₀₀ beam: 99 % at a = 1.52 w, 99.99 % at a = 2.15 w. The medium is uniform and lossless, and widths are 1/e² intensity values throughout.

Fluorescence excitation and detection

Choose an excitation source (an LED, a CW laser or a pulsed laser) and a fluorophore, then emission filters and a detector. Read how strongly the source excites it, how much of its emission is detected, how well the source light is blocked, the crosstalk from a second label, and the excitation rate and saturation of one molecule.

Filters multiply: the stack passes the product of their transmissions.

    Fluorescent marker additional information

    Excitation rate and saturation of one molecule

    From the laser power at the sample: the intensity in a focus or a widefield field, how often one molecule absorbs, how close it is to saturation, and how many of its photons reach the detector [3][5].

      Excitation by the source

      A fluorophore absorbs a photon of wavelength λ with a probability set by its decadic molar extinction coefficient ε(λ), the number spectroscopists quote. For one molecule it is an absorption cross-section σ [3]:

      σ(λ)=ln⁡10⋅103 cm3 L−1NA ε(λ)≈3.82×10−21 cm2×ε(λ)M−1 cm−1\sigma(\lambda)=\frac{\ln 10\cdot 10^{3}\ \mathrm{cm^3\,L^{-1}}}{N_A}\,\varepsilon(\lambda)\approx 3.82\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{cm^2}\times\frac{\varepsilon(\lambda)}{\mathrm{M^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}}σ(λ)=NA​ln10⋅103 cm3L−1​ε(λ)≈3.82×10−21 cm2×M−1cm−1ε(λ)​

      FPbase lists ε at the absorption peak λpeak, and an excitation spectrum X(λ): the fluorescence measured while the excitation wavelength is scanned. For one emitting species the excitation and absorption spectra have the same shape, because the quantum yield does not depend on which absorption band was excited (Kasha–Vavilov) [3]. The value at a CW laser line λ0 therefore scales ε:

      ε(λ0)=εpeak X(λ0)X(λpeak),B=εpeak ΦF,B(λ0)=ε(λ0) ΦF\varepsilon(\lambda_0)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{peak}}\,\frac{X(\lambda_0)}{X(\lambda_{\mathrm{peak}})},\qquad B=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{peak}}\,\Phi_F,\qquad B(\lambda_0)=\varepsilon(\lambda_0)\,\Phi_Fε(λ0​)=εpeak​X(λpeak​)X(λ0​)​,B=εpeak​ΦF​,B(λ0​)=ε(λ0​)ΦF​

      An LED and a pulsed laser have a spectrum w(λ), power per nm. The page takes the same shapes the canvas’s LED source and pulsed laser emit: an LED is a Gaussian band of FWHM Δλ, and a pulse is transform-limited and Gaussian, so its bandwidth follows from its duration τp [14]. Since photons per nm go as w(λ)·λ, the relative excitation, and with it ε and σ, is then averaged over the source’s photons:

      Δλpulse=0.441 λ02c τp,Xˉ=∫w(λ) λ X(λ) dλX(λpeak)∫w(λ) λ dλ\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{pulse}}=\frac{0.441\,\lambda_0^2}{c\,\tau_p},\qquad \bar X=\frac{\int w(\lambda)\,\lambda\,X(\lambda)\,d\lambda}{X(\lambda_{\mathrm{peak}})\int w(\lambda)\,\lambda\,d\lambda}Δλpulse​=cτp​0.441λ02​​,Xˉ=X(λpeak​)∫w(λ)λdλ∫w(λ)λX(λ)dλ​

      X̄, or X(λ0)/X(λpeak) for a line, is the “relative excitation” the page reports, and ΦF is the fluorescence quantum yield. The molecular brightness B compares fluorophores at their own peaks; B at the source compares them under the light you have. The excitation curve in the first graph is X(λ) itself, so the best of the sources you have can be read off it. A few proteins have only an absorption spectrum on FPbase; it is used the same way.

      Filters, detector and the detected fraction

      The emission spectrum E(λ) passes a stack of filters, whose transmissions multiply, and reaches a detector of quantum efficiency Q(λ). The page reports the fraction of the emitted photons the filters pass, and the fraction that is then counted, over the whole emission spectrum:

      T(λ)=∏iTi(λ),ηT=∫E(λ) T(λ) dλ∫E(λ) dλT(\lambda)=\prod_i T_i(\lambda),\qquad \eta_T=\frac{\int E(\lambda)\,T(\lambda)\,d\lambda}{\int E(\lambda)\,d\lambda}T(λ)=i∏​Ti​(λ),ηT​=∫E(λ)dλ∫E(λ)T(λ)dλ​
      ηD=∫E(λ) T(λ) Q(λ) dλ∫E(λ) dλ,Qˉ=ηDηT\eta_D=\frac{\int E(\lambda)\,T(\lambda)\,Q(\lambda)\,d\lambda}{\int E(\lambda)\,d\lambda},\qquad \bar Q=\frac{\eta_D}{\eta_T}ηD​=∫E(λ)dλ∫E(λ)T(λ)Q(λ)dλ​,Qˉ​=ηT​ηD​​

      E(λ) is taken per unit wavelength, as FPbase stores it. A generic filter has peak transmission Tp in its passband and 10−OD outside it, joined by logistic edges whose 10–90 % width Δ you set (0 is an ideal step). Bandpass and notch filters put their edges at the centre ± half the width, so a “525/50” bandpass is centre 525 nm, width 50 nm. The width must be at least three edge widths: narrower, the two edges overlap and the band reaches neither its peak transmission nor its FWHM; from three edge widths on, both hold to 0.3 %:

      Ti(λ)=Tb+(Tp−Tb) S(λ),Tb=10−OD,s(λ;λe)=[1+e−2ln⁡9 (λ−λe)/Δ]−1T_i(\lambda)=T_b+(T_p-T_b)\,S(\lambda),\quad T_b=10^{-\mathrm{OD}},\qquad s(\lambda;\lambda_e)=\Big[1+e^{-2\ln 9\,(\lambda-\lambda_e)/\Delta}\Big]^{-1}Ti​(λ)=Tb​+(Tp​−Tb​)S(λ),Tb​=10−OD,s(λ;λe​)=[1+e−2ln9(λ−λe​)/Δ]−1
      SLP=s(λ;λe),SSP=1−s(λ;λe)S_{\mathrm{LP}}=s(\lambda;\lambda_e),\qquad S_{\mathrm{SP}}=1-s(\lambda;\lambda_e)SLP​=s(λ;λe​),SSP​=1−s(λ;λe​)
      SBP=s(λ;c−W2)[1−s(λ;c+W2)],Snotch=1−SBPS_{\mathrm{BP}}=s\big(\lambda;c-\tfrac W2\big)\Big[1-s\big(\lambda;c+\tfrac W2\big)\Big],\qquad S_{\mathrm{notch}}=1-S_{\mathrm{BP}}SBP​=s(λ;c−2W​)[1−s(λ;c+2W​)],Snotch​=1−SBP​

      The emission side of a dichroic beamsplitter acts like a longpass with weak blocking (OD 1–2). A filter you have measured, or downloaded from its maker, can be loaded as a two-column file (wavelength in nm, then transmission); it is read in your browser and never uploaded, and outside the file’s range its transmission is taken as zero. Its values are read as the file’s header states (a “%” or “percent” in it), otherwise as percent when one exceeds 1.5; choose fractions or percent to override that. The laser line itself is reported as the stack’s transmission and optical density OD = −log10T at the laser wavelength. Back-scattered and reflected laser light can be many orders of magnitude stronger than the fluorescence, which is why emission filters block the line at OD 6 or more.

      A second fluorophore

      With a second label in the sample, the page evaluates it with the same laser and detection path: how strongly the laser excites it, how much of its emission is detected, and its signal relative to the main fluorophore at equal concentrations,

      X=ε2(λL) ΦF,2 ηD,2ε1(λL) ΦF,1 ηD,1X=\frac{\varepsilon_2(\lambda_L)\,\Phi_{F,2}\,\eta_{D,2}}{\varepsilon_1(\lambda_L)\,\Phi_{F,1}\,\eta_{D,1}}X=ε1​(λL​)ΦF,1​ηD,1​ε2​(λL​)ΦF,2​ηD,2​​

      X is the bleed-through of label 2 into this channel. The collection geometry is the same for both and cancels. It ignores energy transfer between the labels and any difference in their environment.

      Excitation rate and saturation

      For a diffraction-limited focus the page takes the peak of the Airy pattern of a uniformly filled pupil, I(v) = I0[2J1(v)/v]² with v = 2π·NA·r/λ, whose total power fixes I0 [4]; widefield illumination is a uniform field of diameter D. An LED cannot be focused to a diffraction-limited spot, its light being spatially incoherent, so with an LED only widefield is offered. For pulses, P and I are averages over time. A short pulse has a broad spectrum, and each wavelength focuses to its own Airy peak, π NA² P(λ)/λ²; at the centre of the focus the page therefore weights the cross-section by that, which changes it from the average at λ0 by the factor κ below. κ is 1 for a line; for a broad band it depends on where the absorption sits within it: 1.278 for EGFP excited by a 5 fs pulse at 560 nm, whose absorption lies on the band’s blue side, but 0.893 for mCherry excited by a 5 fs pulse at 488 nm, whose absorption lies on the red side:

      I0=π NA2 Pλ2  (focus),I=4PπD2  (widefield)I_0=\frac{\pi\,\mathrm{NA}^2\,P}{\lambda^2}\ \ \text{(focus)},\qquad I=\frac{4P}{\pi D^2}\ \ \text{(widefield)}I0​=λ2πNA2P​  (focus),I=πD24P​  (widefield)
      κ=λ02 ∫w(λ) X(λ) λ−1 dλ∫w(λ) X(λ) λ dλ\kappa=\lambda_0^2\,\frac{\int w(\lambda)\,X(\lambda)\,\lambda^{-1}\,d\lambda}{\int w(\lambda)\,X(\lambda)\,\lambda\,d\lambda}κ=λ02​∫w(λ)X(λ)λdλ∫w(λ)X(λ)λ−1dλ​

      One molecule is treated as a two-level system with fluorescence lifetime τ [3][5]. Under a CW laser or an LED it absorbs at the rate k = σφ, where φ is the photon flux (with the source’s mean photon energy for a band). In the steady state a fraction N1 of the time is spent excited, and the emission rate R saturates once kτ approaches 1:

      φ=Iλhc,k=σ φ,N1=kτ1+kτ\varphi=\frac{I\lambda}{hc},\qquad k=\sigma\,\varphi,\qquad N_1=\frac{k\tau}{1+k\tau}φ=hcIλ​,k=σφ,N1​=1+kτkτ​
      R=ΦF k1+kτ,Isat=hcλ σ τR=\Phi_F\,\frac{k}{1+k\tau},\qquad I_{\mathrm{sat}}=\frac{hc}{\lambda\,\sigma\,\tau}R=ΦF​1+kτk​,Isat​=λστhc​

      Pulses are taken much shorter than the lifetime and than the period between them (τp at most τ/10 and 1/(10 frep), otherwise the page declines). During a pulse the molecule then does not decay, so a pulse of photon fluence F excites it with probability p = 1 − e−σF; until the next pulse, a period T = 1/frep later, it decays by e−T/τ. In the periodic steady state the fraction excited after each pulse N+ and the emission rate follow, and σF = 1 marks saturation. The signal depends on the energy per pulse, not only on the average power, which is why the repetition rate is asked for: at the same average power, pulsed excitation saturates much sooner than CW. Far below saturation both give the same rate:

      F=Ifrep hν,p=1−e−σF,N+=p1−(1−p) e−T/τF=\frac{I}{f_{\mathrm{rep}}\,h\nu},\qquad p=1-e^{-\sigma F},\qquad N_+=\frac{p}{1-(1-p)\,e^{-T/\tau}}F=frep​hνI​,p=1−e−σF,N+​=1−(1−p)e−T/τp​
      R=frep ΦF N+(1−e−T/τ),Ppeak=2ln⁡2/π Pfrep τpR=f_{\mathrm{rep}}\,\Phi_F\,N_+\big(1-e^{-T/\tau}\big),\qquad P_{\mathrm{peak}}=2\sqrt{\ln 2/\pi}\ \frac{P}{f_{\mathrm{rep}}\,\tau_p}R=frep​ΦF​N+​(1−e−T/τ),Ppeak​=2ln2/π​ frep​τp​P​

      Of the photons emitted, an isotropic emitter sends the fraction ηcoll into an objective of numerical aperture NA in a medium of index n; the detected photon rate per molecule multiplies the emission rate by that and by the spectral fraction ηD from above:

      ηcoll=1−cos⁡θ2,sin⁡θ=NAn,Rdet=R ηcoll ηD\eta_{\mathrm{coll}}=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2},\quad \sin\theta=\frac{\mathrm{NA}}{n},\qquad R_{\mathrm{det}}=R\,\eta_{\mathrm{coll}}\,\eta_Dηcoll​=21−cosθ​,sinθ=nNA​,Rdet​=Rηcoll​ηD​

      The page also gives the average power at the sample that would bring kτ, or σF, to 1 in the chosen geometry. In practice the signal of a real fluorophore stops growing in proportion to the power well before that, because triplet and other dark states fill up and the molecule bleaches; the two-level numbers are an upper bound on the rate, not a prediction of the image.

      The fluorophore library

      The spectra and numbers come from FPbase, the community-edited fluorescent protein database [1]. FPbase states that its data are free of copyright restrictions, for commercial and non-commercial use, with credit to the original authors [2]; each protein’s file names the paper FPbase credits for the protein and, where FPbase records one, the paper its curves come from, listed below. An entry with neither is left out. They are measurements, not modelled. Each curve is kept at FPbase’s 1 nm sampling: negative values (baseline noise) are set to zero and the curve is scaled to its peak. The excitation peak is taken within 5 nm of the absorption maximum FPbase lists, the wavelength its ε refers to, so a protein’s 280 nm aromatic band can rise above it.

      A record is left out when a listed peak lies more than 5 nm from its own curve’s, because the numbers and the curve then disagree: DsRed and mRuby2 (emission 11 and 7 nm apart) are left out for that reason, and GCaMP6f because its record lists peaks of 300 and 800 nm. Proteins for which FPbase stores another protein’s curve are left out too, since the page would show a relative’s measurement: mEGFP and mEmerald (EGFP’s curves), mCitrine and YPet (Citrine’s), EYFP and Venus (Citrine’s emission), Cerulean (ECFP’s) and mClover3 (Clover’s). Calcium indicators appear in both of their states. Dye spectra (Alexa Fluor, ATTO, cyanine and other dyes) are left out for now: on FPbase most record no source, or only a manufacturer, so their redistribution terms are being confirmed first.

      Other collections were checked and not used: PhotochemCAD [10] and the MPI Brain Research spectra viewer [11] state no licence for their spectra, and PubSpectra [12] has none recorded and dates from 2006. Manufacturers’ spectra viewers do not grant redistribution.

      Precision and limits

      The arithmetic is checked against an independent Python reference to 10⁻⁹ for algebra and exact integrals, 10⁻⁵ for the focus, 2×10⁻⁵ for logistic filter edges and 10⁻⁴ for averages over a source band and for pulse trains, where the page integrates by the trapezoidal rule on 0.05 nm (finer for narrow bands) and splits at hard edges so that ideal steps are exact. The pulse-train check integrates the rate equation through finite pulses; the per-pulse model differs from it by about the ratio of pulse duration to lifetime. The detector curves reproduce their datasheets’ tabulated values to the third decimal. These checks say the page evaluates its model correctly; the inputs set its accuracy. Published ε and ΦF of one protein often differ by 10–20 % between papers; spectra depend on pH, temperature and environment, and FPbase does not record whether an emission curve was measured in photons or in energy per wavelength, which matters a little for broad emission. Read the fractions as good to a few percent at best.

      Not modelled:

      • Two- and three-photon excitation. Their spectra are not the one-photon spectrum at λ/2 or λ/3: EGFP’s two-photon peak is near 927 nm, not 976 nm [13].
      • Pulses not much shorter than the lifetime, chirped pulses, stimulated emission, triplet and dark states, photobleaching and photoswitching kinetics.
      • An excitation filter in front of an LED, and an LED’s real, often asymmetric, spectrum: the page uses a Gaussian band.
      • Concentration effects: inner-filter absorption, reabsorption of the emission, quenching and energy transfer between labels.
      • Polarization, dipole orientation, emission near interfaces, the objective’s own transmission and the dichroic’s reflection of the laser.
      • Filter properties beyond the generic shape: angle of incidence, ripple, out-of-band leaks. Load a measured curve for those.
      • Vectorial high-NA focusing, which makes the focus somewhat wider and its peak lower than the scalar Airy value.

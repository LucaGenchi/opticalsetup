Coherent Raman scattering

Solve the pump, Stokes or Raman shift from the other two, then read the CARS and CSRS wavelengths of two-color coherent Raman scattering. Further panels estimate the spectral resolution set by the pulse durations and show which part of a measured reference spectrum that excitation probes.

    Spectral resolution from pulse durations

    For Gaussian pulses that are transform-limited, or equally chirped as in spectral focusing, the excitation bandwidth depends only on the pump and Stokes durations [3].

        Pump, Stokes and the coherent signals

        A pump (νp) and a lower-frequency Stokes beam (νS) drive a vibrational coherence at their difference frequency [1][4]. With vacuum wavelengths in nm, the Raman shift is in cm⁻¹, and any two of pump, Stokes and shift fix the third:

        Δν~=107(1λp−1λS),λS=107107/λp−Δν~,λp=107107/λS+Δν~\Delta\tilde\nu=10^7\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_p}-\frac{1}{\lambda_S}\right),\qquad \lambda_S=\frac{10^7}{10^7/\lambda_p-\Delta\tilde\nu},\qquad \lambda_p=\frac{10^7}{10^7/\lambda_S+\Delta\tilde\nu}Δν~=107(λp​1​−λS​1​),λS​=107/λp​−Δν~107​,λp​=107/λS​+Δν~107​
        νRaman [THz]=0.0299792458  Δν~ [cm−1]\nu_{\mathrm{Raman}}\,[\mathrm{THz}]=0.0299792458\;\Delta\tilde\nu\,[\mathrm{cm}^{-1}]νRaman​[THz]=0.0299792458Δν~[cm−1]

        The same two beams also read the coherence out. A pump photon scattering off it gains the Raman energy, which gives coherent anti-Stokes Raman scattering (CARS) on the blue side of the pump. A Stokes photon losing it gives coherent Stokes Raman scattering (CSRS) on the red side of the Stokes beam [1][2]. Stimulated Raman scattering (SRS) is read out at the pump and Stokes wavelengths themselves, as a loss or a gain.

        νCARS=νp+(νp−νS)=2νp−νS,νCSRS=νS−(νp−νS)=2νS−νp\nu_{\mathrm{CARS}}=\nu_p+(\nu_p-\nu_S)=2\nu_p-\nu_S,\qquad \nu_{\mathrm{CSRS}}=\nu_S-(\nu_p-\nu_S)=2\nu_S-\nu_pνCARS​=νp​+(νp​−νS​)=2νp​−νS​,νCSRS​=νS​−(νp​−νS​)=2νS​−νp​

        Spectral resolution

        Mohseni, Polzer and Hellerer derive the spectral resolution of spectral-focusing coherent Raman imaging from the pump and Stokes durations τp and τS, the intensity FWHM of Gaussian pulses, and state that it holds for transform-limited pulses as well as for equally chirped ones [3], Eq. (5):

        Δν~=22 ln⁡2πcτp−2+τS−2≈20.8 ps cm−1τp−2+τS−2\Delta\tilde\nu=\frac{2\sqrt2\,\ln2}{\pi c}\sqrt{\tau_p^{-2}+\tau_S^{-2}}\approx 20.8\ \mathrm{ps\,cm^{-1}}\sqrt{\tau_p^{-2}+\tau_S^{-2}}Δν~=πc22​ln2​τp−2​+τS−2​​≈20.8 pscm−1τp−2​+τS−2​​

        With equal linear chirps the instantaneous pump–Stokes difference frequency is constant in time, so the chirps cancel in the beat and only the chirped envelopes matter. For spectral focusing, enter those chirped durations. A chirp mismatch adds a broadening term that this panel does not include (Eq. (8) of [3]). For Gaussian fields the value equals the FWHM of the amplitude spectrum of the driving term EpES*. The FWHM of its power spectrum is smaller by √2. The independent reference recomputes the amplitude-spectrum width numerically, for transform-limited and equally chirped pulses.

        Reference spectra

        The spectra are measurements published by others under licences that allow redistribution with credit, chosen so that nothing conflicts with this project’s GPL. They are not modelled. Three sources qualify:

        • Solvents and reagents: Flanagan and Glavin, spontaneous Raman of 31 neat liquids at 785 nm, about 1 cm⁻¹ resolution, CC BY 4.0 [5][6]. Their methyl isobutyl ketone and 4-methyl-2-pentanone are the same compound and appear once.
        • Pharmaceutical excipients: Bērziņš et al., FT-Raman spectra of the excipients as supplied, solids and a few liquids, CC BY 4.0 [7][8]; one spectrum per substance, without repeated grades and suppliers.
        • Minerals: the Raman Open Database, public domain (CC0) [9][10]. Only spectra contributed to ROD directly are used, because many ROD entries republish RRUFF spectra whose own terms are not CC0. Entries flagged by the ROD maintainers, or whose main band lies more than about 3 cm⁻¹ from its accepted position, are left out.

        Other collections were checked and not used: SDBS (all rights reserved), the Open Specy library (merged from contributors without a per-spectrum licence) and RamanBioLib (ODbL share-alike, and spectra digitized from published figures). Lipid spectra are not included yet; measured lipid spectra, including stimulated Raman (SRS) spectra, can be added later and will be labelled as such. SRS is proportional to the spontaneous Raman cross-section but is a different measurement.

        Every spectrum is processed the same way. Replicates are averaged. The spectrum is cropped to the source’s usable range (150–3150 cm⁻¹ for the solvents, as their authors recommend) and averaged into 2 cm⁻¹ bins. A straight baseline through the first and last 10 cm⁻¹ is subtracted, then the spectrum is scaled to its strongest band. The major bands of eight solvents (ethanol, methanol, acetone, acetonitrile, chloroform, cyclohexane, DMSO and toluene), checked against the band table their source publishes, agree to within one bin. Band heights depend on the instrument response, the excitation wavelength and the sample state, so compare positions rather than heights across sources.

        Precision and limits

        Wavelength inputs and inferred pump or Stokes wavelengths are limited to 100–20000 nm, and shifts to positive values up to 10000 cm⁻¹. Equal or reversed pump and Stokes, impossible shifts and out-of-scope inferred wavelengths give no result. CARS and CSRS may lie outside the input interval. CSRS is unavailable when the Raman frequency is not below the Stokes frequency. An independent reference checks all three modes and the resolution formula to 10⁻⁹ relative; the paper’s 20.8 ps·cm⁻¹ prefactor anchors the units.

        Frequency relations and a Gaussian bandwidth estimate only. Raman cross-sections, linewidths, the nonresonant background, phase matching, polarization, pulse overlap, signal levels and non-Gaussian pulse shapes are outside this model.
